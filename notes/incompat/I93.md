# I93. Intraband (Drude) term of the optical conductivity x tetrahedra

Status: done after the fix round (below). The Drude term of a tetrahedron run
(`tetrahedra`, `tetrahedra_lin`, `tetrahedra_opt`) is built from `d wg_nk / d E_F` of the
tetrahedron weights. On fcc aluminium the linear kind sits 0.016 to 0.034 eV below the
`degauss^2` extrapolation of the Gaussian-smeared plasma frequency (about 12.466 eV) on
every grid from 40^3 to 72^3, and that extrapolation is itself biased high by up to 0.07
eV, so the agreement is resolved to about 0.03 to 0.05 eV and no tighter. What converges
cleanly with the mesh is the spread between the three tetrahedron kinds. Still in place,
each with a test: `intraband = False` and fixed occupations give no Drude term; a
tetrahedron run on an explicit k-list raises `tetrahedra_for`'s "needs an automatic k-point
grid".

The convergence parameter is the k-mesh, and three statements hold on these six grids.

1. **The linear kind's gap to the fit levels off, it does not fall.** It is -0.016,
   -0.017, -0.029, -0.034 and -0.022 eV on 40 to 72 cubed. The fitted limit converges to
   about 12.466 and the linear kind settles at 12.43 to 12.44 (12.4302, 12.4692, 12.4320,
   12.4328, 12.4430). The 48^3 pair, which the first version of this record built its
   statement on, is a k-mesh fluctuation where both numbers are high by about 0.02 eV.
2. **The `degauss^2` extrapolation is biased high.** On 80^3 (`v2b.log`, `I93-ver2/b80.log`)
   Methfessel-Paxton reads 12.4735, 12.4470, 12.4344, 12.4234 and 12.3915 at `degauss`
   0.04, 0.03, 0.02, 0.015 and 0.01, and Gaussian 0.01 reads 12.4270; a `degauss^4` fit
   over three consecutive widths drifts from 12.433 to 12.398 as the widths fall, where
   the smallest widths are no longer resolved by the mesh. So the `degauss -> 0` value is
   known only to within roughly 12.39 to 12.44 eV (or 12.47 for the Gaussian fit), which is
   as wide as the 0.02 to 0.03 eV gap, and the comparison resolves 0.03 to 0.05 eV. The
   linear kind lies inside that window on every grid from 40^3.
3. **The three kinds converge towards each other.** Linear minus Bloechl is 0.136, 0.075,
   0.068, 0.040 and 0.029 eV on 40, 48, 56, 64 and 72 cubed; optimised minus linear is
   0.037 on 40^3 and 0.005 on 48^3. This is the convergence the approximations predict,
   since all three integrate the same Fermi-surface quantity exactly as the mesh grows.

The slow test's tolerance, 0.05 eV, passes for the linear kind on all six grids (worst
-0.034 on 64^3); its pin `limit == 12.4858 +- 5e-3` is labelled in the test as a 48^3
regression pin, not the converged limit.

**Bloechl's corrected weights are still converging.** `tetrahedra` reads 12.2947, 12.3937,
12.3642, 12.3933 and 12.4141 on 40, 48, 56, 64 and 72 cubed, and approaches the linear
kind as the gap above says. The first version of this record said it had converged to a
different number on the evidence of 48^3 and 64^3 agreeing to 4e-4 eV; that agreement is a
coincidence, and the statement and the reason offered for it are withdrawn. The slow test's
0.15 eV for Bloechl is a 48^3 statement (it is -0.151 on 40^3).

**Bloechl's decomposition is not cubic.** On the whole 8^3 grid with `nosym`, the plasma
tensor of `tetrahedra` has off-diagonal 2.05 eV and a diagonal spread of 0.68 eV, where
`tetrahedra_lin` and `tetrahedra_opt` give 9e-5 and 4e-10. `tetra_init`'s hardwired
microcell cut breaks the cubic group; the trace is unaffected (wedge against whole grid
`trace/3` of `wp^2` agrees to 3e-10 relative on 4^3, measured on this workstation), and on
the wedge the group average restores the cubic form. The linear and optimised kinds give
the same number on the wedge and on the whole 8^3 grid to the printed digits (11.732272,
11.148130), which is the wedge completion checked for this term.

The term switched off: `intraband = False` gives `wp = 0` exactly, on 48^3 (this record's
run) and on 40^3 (`v1o.log`), against 12.43 to 12.47 eV with it on, which is also what the
code returned before the refusal was written. A plasma sum with `w_k` applied twice (the
tetrahedron weights already carry the zone measure) would be low by a factor of the grid
size, and the unit test below fails on it.

## What was missing

The entry's term was right: a Fermi-surface delta from the tetrahedron weights. It was
already written, `scf/tetrahedra.py:tetrahedron_fermi_weights`, and used by
`Calculation.ldos_weights`; what was missing was its use in
`response/conductivity.py:_fermi_surface_delta`. Three things the entry did not say:

1. **The normalisation.** The tetrahedron weights carry the zone measure and the spin
   degeneracy (`spin / ntetra`), and `_plasma_sum` multiplies by `w_k` itself, so the delta
   is the weight divided by `w_k`. On a wedge `w_k` is the orbit's share and the
   representative collects the corners of its whole orbit, so the quotient is right there
   too.
2. **Rule D4.** `_plasma_sum` contracts `delta_n v^nm v^mn` over each multiplet, which is
   invariant under a rotation inside the multiplet only when `delta_n` is equal across it.
   A smeared delta is a function of the energy; a tetrahedron weight is not, and Bloechl's
   are not averaged at all by `tetra.f90`. The delta is block-averaged over the plasma
   sum's own multiplets (`_multiplet_average`, the partition `_average_degenerate` makes).
3. **Memory.** The optimised kind's scatter holds `(ntetra, nbnd, 4, 20)` reals, 3.8 GB at
   `nbnd = 9` on 48^3 before the `jvp`, and run 3 (`b1a82a13`) was killed at D22's 6 GB cap
   on that row. The weights of one band read only that band's corners, so the delta is now
   computed one band at a time over the bands whose interpolation energies straddle `E_F`
   (the fix round corrected this test for the optimised kind), with the degenerate average
   redone on the whole set; the 48^3 row then ran. On 64^3 the `tetrahedra_opt` row was
   still killed at 6 GB (`h4b.log`); which step holds the memory there was not measured,
   and `calculation.occupations`, which builds the interband weights of all `nbnd` bands
   with the same scatter, is the candidate. The 64^3 Gaussian rows were therefore run
   separately (`h5.log`, `I93_SCHEMES=gaussian`).

QE and Elk: neither has it. Elk's `dielectric.f90` builds `wplas` from `sdelta(stype, x) /
swidth` (`:100-102`) and has no tetrahedra. QE's `epsilon.x` turns its intraband branch on
for `ltetra` (`PP/src/epsilon.f90:321`, `metalcalc = .TRUE.`) and then divides
`w0gauss((et - efermi)/degauss)` by `degauss` (`:511-516`), which a tetrahedron run leaves
at zero unless the input sets one, so there is no tetrahedron Drude term to compare with.

## Fix round (2026-10-19)

The adversarial verifier refuted the first version (`f8891a9d`) on three points. Each is
answered below; the production numbers on Al did not change.

1. **The crossing filter was wrong for `tetrahedra_opt`.** The band-at-a-time loop kept
   only the bands with `min_k e_nk <= E_F <= max_k e_nk`. That is exact for Bloechl and the
   linear kind, whose interpolation energies are the grid eigenvalues, and false for the
   optimised kind, whose 20-point `wlsm` combinations have negative entries and reach past
   a band's grid extrema. On a 6^3 grid with a second band whose grid minimum sits 1e-3 Ry
   above `E_F`, `sum_k w_k sum_n delta` read 5.4401 against `tetrahedron_dos(E_F)` = 5.4977
   (1.0 per cent missing; 5.4794 at a 5e-3 gap, 5.4419 at 2e-2). The filter now tests each
   band's own `_corner_energies(table, e[:, n:n+1])`, which costs one `(ntetra, 1, 4)`
   array per band. A band left out is then exactly zero for every kind, so the memory fix
   stands and nothing is approximated. New unit test
   `test_the_tetrahedron_drude_delta_keeps_a_band_just_above_the_level`: with the old
   filter restored it fails for `tetrahedra_opt` (5.440146538948126 against
   5.497714549324182), and passes for all three kinds with the fix. The level in that test
   is `0.05 + 1e-7`, off every grid eigenvalue, because at a level that sits on one the
   `jvp` of the piecewise weights is one-sided at a kink and the identity breaks for an
   unrelated reason (verifier's `offtie.py`: 5.5144 against 5.9877 on nosym 6^3 at exactly
   0.05; pre-existing in `tetrahedron_fermi_weights`, shared with `ldos_weights`, not
   reachable from a bisected `E_F`). On Al the filter has no effect: the verifier's
   filter-free mutant (`/l/ladovj1/review/incompat/I93-ver/m-all.log`) is identical to the
   reference (`m-ref.log`) to the printed six decimals on 16, 24 and 32 cubed for all three
   kinds, and the new filter admits a superset of the old one's bands and a subset of all
   of them, so on those grids it gives the same numbers. Checked at 16 to 32 cubed only.
2. **The D4 average was not tested where it is applied.** Replacing
   `return _multiplet_average(delta, energies, tol)` by `return delta` left all unit tests
   passing. New unit test `test_the_tetrahedron_drude_delta_is_averaged_where_it_is_built`
   goes through `_fermi_surface_delta`: two crossing bands sorted per k are exactly
   degenerate on the planes `k1 = +-k2` of a nosym 6^3 grid, the returned delta must be
   equal across every degenerate pair (to 1e-12), the raw per-band weights must not be (by
   more than 1 per cent of their largest value), and the sum over states must be unchanged.
   With `return delta` it fails for all three kinds. On Al the average moves nothing to the
   printed digits (verifier's `m-nod4.log` against `m-ref.log`, 16 to 32 cubed); the unit
   test is where it is shown to act.
3. **The convergence statements did not hold.** The gap to the smeared limit was said to
   fall with the mesh, Bloechl was said to have converged to 4e-4 eV on a different number,
   and the guide text quoted 12.49 eV and "within 0.02 eV", all from the 48^3 point. With
   the verifiers' 40, 56 and 72 cubed rows (and 80^3 smeared rows) merged into the table in
   "The number", the record now says what the six grids support: the linear kind's gap
   levels off at -0.02 to -0.03 eV, the `degauss^2` limit is biased high and the
   `degauss -> 0` value is known only to 12.39 to 12.47 eV, Bloechl is still converging
   towards the linear kind (0.136 to 0.029 eV from 40^3 to 72^3), and the kinds converge
   towards each other. The guide replacement below was rewritten accordingly and names
   both memory kills (`tetrahedra_opt` on 64^3, `tetrahedra_lin` on 80^3, `v2a.log`, exit
   137 before its first row). The slow test keeps its assertions (the 0.05 eV tolerance
   passes for the linear kind on all six grids); its docstring was rewritten and the
   12.4858 pin is labelled a 48^3 regression pin.

The minor item on `_multiplet_average`'s Python double loop (about 2 million iterations on
a nosym 64^3 grid, harmless on a wedge) was not changed. No new physics run was made in the
fix round; the convergence numbers are the verifiers' D22 runs at `fc29dc71`, whose
production path is unchanged by the fix as point 1 says.

## The user guide

`docs/guide/optics.md`, the "Limits" note, delete:

> Its Drude term gives an error with tetrahedra, which have no smeared delta function at
> the Fermi level, so you use smearing, or `intraband=False` to read the interband part
> alone.

and write in its place:

> With tetrahedra the Drude term uses the tetrahedron weights at the Fermi level. So it
> needs no smearing width. We tested it on aluminium, on meshes from 40x40x40 to 72x72x72.
> There `tetrahedra_lin` gives a plasma frequency of 12.43 to 12.44 eV. The smeared value,
> taken to zero width, is between 12.39 and 12.47 eV. So the two agree to about 0.03 to
> 0.05 eV. The three tetrahedron variants come closer to each other as the mesh gets
> denser. The Bloechl variant, `occupations = 'tetrahedra'`, converges the slowest. It is
> 0.03 eV below `tetrahedra_lin` on 72x72x72, so we suggest `tetrahedra_lin` or
> `tetrahedra_opt`. On the whole grid with `nosym`, the Bloechl variant also gives a tensor
> that is not cubic on a cubic crystal. This comes from the way it cuts the mesh into
> tetrahedra. The memory grows fast with the mesh. With 6 GB, `tetrahedra_opt` stopped at
> 64x64x64 and `tetrahedra_lin` at 80x80x80.
