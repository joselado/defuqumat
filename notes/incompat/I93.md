# I93. Intraband (Drude) term of the optical conductivity x tetrahedra

Status: done. The Drude term of a tetrahedron run (`tetrahedra`, `tetrahedra_lin`,
`tetrahedra_opt`) is built from `d wg_nk / d E_F` of the tetrahedron weights, and on fcc
aluminium its plasma frequency lands on the `degauss -> 0` limit of the Gaussian-smeared one
to 0.011 to 0.034 eV of 12.47 (linear and optimised) on the two densest grids, with the gap
falling with the mesh. Still in place, each with a test: `intraband = False` and fixed
occupations give no Drude term; a tetrahedron run on an explicit k-list raises
`tetrahedra_for`'s "needs an automatic k-point grid".

The convergence parameter is the k-mesh, and the discrepancy falls the way the
approximations say: the tetrahedron kinds spread by 0.94 eV on 8^3, 0.21 on 32^3, 0.08 on
48^3 and 0.04 on 64^3, and their gap to the smeared limit falls from 32^3 to 48^3 for all
three. On 48^3 and 64^3 the linear and optimised kinds agree with the limit to 0.011 to 0.034
eV, 0.1 to 0.3 per cent, which is the size of the limit's own uncertainty: the smeared value
at `degauss = 0.02` still moves 0.06 eV between 32^3 and 64^3, the fit residual is 0.25 to
0.4 eV^2 on 156, and below 0.02 the smeared value stops following `degauss^2` (on 48^3 it
turns up, on 64^3 it keeps falling to 12.37), because the grid no longer resolves the width.
The slow test's tolerance, 0.05 eV, is set from the 48^3 and 64^3 pairs.

**Bloechl's corrected weights converge to a different number.** `tetrahedra` reads 12.3937
on 48^3 and 12.3933 on 64^3, converged to 4e-4 eV, and sits 0.07 to 0.09 eV (0.6 per cent)
below the smeared limit and below the two other kinds. The likely reason is that the
derivative of Bloechl's curvature correction is not a delta function times a linearly
interpolated corner value, so for a Fermi-surface integral of `v_a v_b` it is not the
integral it is for the electron count; that reason is not measured. It is the same choice
`Calculation.ldos_weights` already made for the LDOS. The slow test holds it to 0.15 eV.

**Bloechl's decomposition is not cubic.** On the whole 8^3 grid with `nosym`, the plasma
tensor of `tetrahedra` has off-diagonal 2.05 eV and a diagonal spread of 0.68 eV, where
`tetrahedra_lin` and `tetrahedra_opt` give 9e-5 and 4e-10. `tetra_init`'s hardwired
microcell cut breaks the cubic group; the trace is unaffected (wedge against whole grid
`trace/3` of `wp^2` agrees to 3e-10 relative on 4^3, measured on this workstation), and on
the wedge the group average restores the cubic form. The linear and optimised kinds give
the same number on the wedge and on the whole 8^3 grid to the printed digits (11.732272,
11.148130), which is the wedge completion checked for this term.

The term switched off: `intraband = False` gives `wp = 0` exactly, against 12.47 eV, which
is also what the code returned before the refusal was written. A plasma sum with `w_k`
applied twice (the tetrahedron weights already carry the zone measure) would be low by a
factor of the grid size, and the unit test below fails on it.

## What was missing

The entry's term was right: a Fermi-surface delta from the tetrahedron weights. It was
already written, `scf/tetrahedra.py:tetrahedron_fermi_weights`, and used by
`Calculation.ldos_weights`; what was missing was its use in
`response/conductivity.py:_fermi_surface_delta`. Three things the entry did not say:

1. **The normalisation.** The tetrahedron weights carry the zone measure and the spin
   degeneracy (`spin / ntetra`), and `_plasma_sum` multiplies by `w_k` itself, so the delta
   is the weight divided by `w_k`. On a wedge `w_k` is the orbit's share and the
   representative collects the corners of its whole orbit, so the quotient is right there
   too.
2. **Rule D4.** `_plasma_sum` contracts `delta_n v^nm v^mn` over each multiplet, which is
   invariant under a rotation inside the multiplet only when `delta_n` is equal across it.
   A smeared delta is a function of the energy; a tetrahedron weight is not, and Bloechl's
   are not averaged at all by `tetra.f90`. The delta is block-averaged over the plasma
   sum's own multiplets (`_multiplet_average`, the partition `_average_degenerate` makes).
3. **Memory.** The optimised kind's scatter holds `(ntetra, nbnd, 4, 20)` reals, 3.8 GB at
   `nbnd = 9` on 48^3 before the `jvp`, and run 3 (`b1a82a13`) was killed at D22's 6 GB cap
   on that row. The weights of one band read only that band's corners, so the delta is now
   computed one band at a time over the bands that cross `E_F`, with the degenerate average
   redone on the whole set; the 48^3 row then ran. On 64^3 the `tetrahedra_opt` row was
   still killed at 6 GB (`h4b.log`); which step holds the memory there was not measured,
   and `calculation.occupations`, which builds the interband weights of all `nbnd` bands
   with the same scatter, is the candidate. The 64^3 Gaussian rows were therefore run
   separately (`h5.log`, `I93_SCHEMES=gaussian`).

QE and Elk: neither has it. Elk's `dielectric.f90` builds `wplas` from `sdelta(stype, x) /
swidth` (`:100-102`) and has no tetrahedra. QE's `epsilon.x` turns its intraband branch on
for `ltetra` (`PP/src/epsilon.f90:321`, `metalcalc = .TRUE.`) and then divides
`w0gauss((et - efermi)/degauss)` by `degauss` (`:511-516`), which a tetrahedron run leaves
at zero unless the input sets one, so there is no tetrahedron Drude term to compare with.

## The user guide

`docs/guide/optics.md`, the "Limits" note, delete:

> Its Drude term gives an error with tetrahedra, which have no smeared delta function at
> the Fermi level, so you use smearing, or `intraband=False` to read the interband part
> alone.

and write in its place:

> With tetrahedra the Drude term uses the tetrahedron weights at the Fermi level, so it
> needs no smearing width. On aluminium on a 48x48x48 mesh, `tetrahedra_lin` and
> `tetrahedra_opt` give a plasma frequency within 0.02 eV of the smeared value at zero
> width, which is 12.49 eV. The Bloechl variant, `occupations = 'tetrahedra'`, converges
> to a value 0.09 eV lower, so we suggest the other two. On the whole grid with `nosym`
> the Bloechl variant also gives a tensor that is not cubic on a cubic crystal, because of
> the way it cuts the mesh into tetrahedra. On a 64x64x64 mesh `tetrahedra_opt` needed
> more than 6 GB of memory.
