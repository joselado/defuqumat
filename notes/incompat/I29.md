# I29. Source-free exchange-correlation field (`nosource`) x spin spiral: blocked, permanently

No number, and none is possible: the refusal is physics, not a missing term,
and stays. Its message now says why.

**Where the entry was wrong.** It names the missing term as "`div B_xc` in the
laboratory frame against a rotating-frame magnetization", as if the projection
only had to be taken in the right frame. Taken in the laboratory frame, the
projected field is no longer a spiral field. The generalized Bloch theorem needs
`B(r + R) = R_z(q . R) B(r)`, a turn in spin alone, and the projection
`1 - grad (lap)^-1 div` couples the spin components to the spatial directions,
so it does not commute with that turn. On one component: a transverse
`B_+ = b e^{i (G - q).r}` with `G` along `x` and `q` along `z` has
`div B ~ G_x`, and its longitudinal part has a `z` component
`~ G_x q cos((G - q).r)`, which is not lattice periodic. No spiral carries it.
It is the position of spin-orbit coupling: whatever couples spin to space breaks
the combined translation, and the commensurate supercell is the only way.

**Tests** (both in `tests/unit/test_source_free_field.py`, no SCF, run here):
`test_a_spin_spiral_is_refused` fires the guard (the entry said "Test: none");
`test_the_source_free_field_of_a_spiral_is_not_a_spiral` builds exactly that
field on a box read as four primitive cells, projects it, and asserts the
projected `B_z` is nonzero (> 0.1 on a field of 1) and changes under a
translation by one primitive cell (> 0.1).

So this item belongs with T5: "the identity that remains" is that a refusal is
the right answer. Elk was not read.

**User guide**, `docs/guide/magnetism.md`, Limits: in "`nosource` also gives an
error in a collinear run, with a PAW dataset, on a spin spiral, ...", the
spiral stays. If a reason is wanted, add after that sentence: "On a spin spiral
the projection would turn the spins with the spatial direction, so the field is
no longer a spiral, as with spin-orbit coupling."
