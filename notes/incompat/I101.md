# I101. Real-time propagation x static saw-tooth (`tefield`)

Status: done for the frozen potential. A saw-tooth ground state propagates with `potential =
'frozen'` (the default). Measured: `propagate` directly and `Calculator.get_realtime`.
`get_realtime_dielectric`, `get_hhg`, `get_harmonic_orders` and `get_third_harmonic` go
through the same `_solved_states` and `_prepare`, read no `tefield`, and are no longer
refused, but they were not run on a saw-tooth. Still refused, by name: the potential updated in time (`'hartree'`,
`'hxc'`) with a saw-tooth, the frequency-domain hierarchy (`get_nonlinear_spectrum`) with a
saw-tooth, and the Berry-phase field (`lelfield`) on every real-time route.

Code measured: `afffd257` (code identical to the final commit; the commit after it changes
only the test input, the slow test and this note). D22 worktree `/l/ladovj1/w-inc-I101-r`,
logs under `/l/ladovj1/review/incompat/I101/` (`run2.log`, `run3.log`, `run3.json`, harnesses
`harness2.py`, `harness4.py`). One run each, CPU, one core.

## What was missing

The entry's term, "the length gauge (saw-tooth) and the velocity gauge (laser) in one
Hamiltonian", is not a missing term. The saw-tooth is a periodic scalar potential that
`Calculation.potential` puts inside `v_scf` (`scf/driver.py`, the `self.sawtooth` branch),
and `_solved_states` builds the frozen `v_scf` with that call, so the field is already in
the propagated Hamiltonian. The laser couples to the kinetic and nonlocal terms through
`kappa`. The two are different fields in one ordinary Hamiltonian, and nothing in
`propagate` reads `tefield`. What the lift needed:

- `require_a_realtime_regime` no longer calls `refuse_sawtooth`; it keeps that function's
  `lelfield` branch as its own refusal (the Berry-phase operator couples k-points on the
  string, which independent propagation of each k cannot carry).
- `require_a_potential_mode` refuses a saw-tooth for `'hartree'` and `'hxc'`: `_Run`'s
  `'hxc'` closure would take the field inside `U`, so `e_sawtooth` drops out of the
  recorded energy, and with `dipfield` the dipole correction would move with `rho(t)` in
  `'hxc'` and not in `'hartree'`. Neither is measured. Kept refused (the user's question).
- `run_nonlinear_spectrum` refuses `tefield` by name: it shares the state builder whose
  refusal was lifted, and the steady-state solve was not measured on a saw-tooth.
- A real bug on the way: `_chunk_spectrum` reduced an empty nonlocal spectrum on a dataset
  with no projectors (`H.pz-vbc`, `number_of_proj = 0`) and crashed with "zero-size array
  to reduction operation minimum". It now skips the nonlocal bound when `nkb = 0`. This
  hit any real-time run on a projector-free dataset, not only a saw-tooth one.

`Calculator.get_realtime(Kick(0))` on the 12-bohr cell gives 1.6e-12, so no guard is left on
the user's route. With the term off the states are not eigenstates: the excited fraction is
4.5e-3 where with the term it is 1.5e-14.

**Linear response (the entry's "the field-free run plus the static polarization").** The
pulse is `E0 sin^2(pi t/T)` along z, `E0 = 1e-4`, and we take the odd part in `E0`, so
`P(t) = int J dt` is fitted to `alpha E(t)/Omega + b int kappa dt`. The reference is the
frozen-potential polarizability in the length gauge: minus the second central difference of
the band energy in `eamp`, at the same frozen Hxc potential (`fixed_density_states`). It
shares no code with the current. Its own step convergence: 10.01622 (step 1e-3) against
10.01656 (step 2e-3) at 12 bohr, 10.11818 against 10.11865 at 16 bohr, so 3e-5 relative.

The term `b` is needed because a plain projection on `E(t)` grows as `T^2`. At 12 bohr on
1x1x1 it gives 25.1, 71.0 and 254.3 bohr^3 at T = 100, 200, 400 against a static 10.016. The
cause is a Drude-like current proportional to `kappa`. `n = -b Omega` has two parts:

- The band's dispersion through the box, sampled at one k-point: n = 0.0222 on 1x1x1. The
  dispersion is measured directly: `eps(k_z = pi/L) - eps(0)` = 1.09e-3 Ry at 12 bohr. On
  1x1x2 and 1x1x4, n drops to 0.0046, so this part is gone from a two-point mesh along the
  field.
- A remainder of n = 0.0046 to 0.0049. It does not move with the mesh (1x1x2 and 1x1x4
  agree to three digits) or with the box (16 bohr gives 0.0049), and at `eamp = 0` it is
  0.0046. So it is not the saw-tooth's. The likely cause is the fixed sphere at `k + kappa`:
  a truncated basis does not satisfy the diamagnetic sum rule. This is not verified, since
  no `ecutwfc` series was run.

`alpha` from the two-term fit, against the static value:

| cell | T = 100 | T = 200 | T = 400 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 bohr, 1x1x1 (static 10.0162) | 9.859 (-1.6%) | 9.810 (-2.1%) | 10.298 (+2.8%) |
| 12 bohr, 1x1x2 (static 10.0179) | 10.271 (+2.5%) | 10.182 (+1.6%) | 10.158 (+1.4%) |
| 12 bohr, 1x1x4 (static 10.0182) | 10.270 (+2.5%) | 10.182 (+1.6%) | 10.161 (+1.4%) |
| 16 bohr, 1x1x1 (static 10.1182) | 10.200 (+0.8%) | 10.121 (+0.03%) | 10.139 (+0.20%) |

Three convergence parameters. The pulse length T sets the
adiabatic error: at 12 bohr on 1x1x2 (and only there; the 16-bohr 1x1x1 row is not this) it
falls 2.5, 1.6, 1.4 per cent, and 0.88 at T = 800 (verifier), toward about 1.3 per cent. The mesh along the field removes the dispersion
term. The box takes the rest from 1.3-1.4 per cent at 12 bohr to 0.2 per cent at 16 bohr. A
gap between the two gauges that shrinks with the box from 12 bohr is what we expect: the saw-tooth
returns 4.1 bohr from an atom at 12 bohr and 5.7 bohr at 16, while the vector potential is
uniform everywhere. The 16-bohr 1x1x1 series in T is not monotonic (0.8, 0.03, 0.2 per cent).
This was read as a floor at first, and the reading is wrong: it is the response leaving the
linear regime as `kappa` grows with T, which the fix round measured (below), so the
+0.20 per cent is partly a cancellation between the adiabatic error and that nonlinearity. The 1x1x1 12-bohr row is not monotonic, because there the Drude term is
24 times the polarization at T = 400 and the fit separates them poorly.

**The term switched off.** The same 12-bohr 1x1x2 run at T = 400 with `v_scf - v_saw` gives
`alpha` = 6.41 (-36%). Its even part in `E0` is 1.1e-4, against 2e-12 with the term in,
because the states move with no pulse at all.

**The saw-tooth's own effect on alpha.** On 12 bohr 1x1x2, the static `alpha` goes from
9.9556 at `eamp = 0` to 10.0179 at `eamp = 0.01`, so +0.0623 (0.63%; H2 is centrosymmetric,
so this is `gamma F^2/2`). The real-time `alpha` at T = 400 goes from 10.0879 to 10.1583, so
+0.0704. The two gauges see the same field-induced shift to 0.008, which is inside the
1.4 per cent gauge gap at this box size (0.14 bohr^3). This shift is smaller than the
T-convergence discrepancy, so the linear arm alone could not tell `eamp = 0.01` from
`eamp = 0`. What shows that the term is in the propagated Hamiltonian is the
stationarity pair and the term-off arm.

## Fix round (2026-10-19)

**What was refuted.** The verifier refuted the linear-response arm only (check g). On the
committed 16-bohr 1x1x1 cell at `E0 = 1e-4`, one more doubling of T broke the slow test's
1 per cent: `alpha` = 10.745 at T = 800 (+6.2 per cent) and 19.91 at T = 1600 (+97 per

the run leaves the linear regime as `kappa_final = E0 T/2` grows, because on one k-point the
band's dispersion through the box is not parabolic in `kappa`. The "floor" reading of the
16-bohr series was wrong, and the "4 per doubling" sentence described 12 bohr 1x1x2, not
the committed cell. A minor gap: `hierarchy_orders` called directly, bypassing
`run_nonlinear_spectrum`, was not guarded against `tefield`. The guard lift, the
stationarity pair, the two refusals and the `_chunk_spectrum` fix held, and are left alone.

The T = 400, `E0 = 1e-4` point reproduces the verifier's 10.1253, and T = 800, `E0 = 1e-4`
its 10.1329. The deviation is a sum of two parts. The adiabatic part falls with T and

T = 800 and `E0 = 2.5e-5`, puts the gauge gap at 16 bohr 1x1x2 at 0.016 per cent, about half

## The user's question (taken the conservative way)

Should the potential updated in time carry the saw-tooth? Two choices are open. One is to
keep `e_sawtooth` in the recorded energy and freeze the field outside `U`. The other is to
also move the dipole correction with `rho(t)` under `dipfield`. Neither was measured, so
both modes stay refused.
