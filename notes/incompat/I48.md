# I48. Force-theorem, torque and relaxed anisotropy off the system's axis x symmetry-reduced k-set

**Status: partial.** The force theorem is lifted with a number. The relaxed route, the
torque and the frozen expectation value keep their refusal: the relaxed route's lift was
in code on this branch without a passing check and has been put back (see "Relaxed
route" below).

## The numbers

Tetragonal cobalt (`co-tetragonal-anisotropy-soc.in`, 3x3x2 unshifted, 18 points with
`nosym`), and the same input with `nosym` removed, so each direction is sampled on its
own magnetic wedge: 6 points along `z`, 8 along `x`, `(1,1,0)` and `(1,0,1)`, 10 along
`(0.3, 0.5, 0.8)`. One D22 run of each harness (`aniso-ft.log`, `aniso-relaxed2.log`).

**Force theorem**, one frozen density (the scalar-relativistic SCF) in both arms:

| direction | `E_band` whole (Ry) | wedge - whole |
|---|---|---|
| z | 3.049461629499 | -4.8e-11 meV |
| x | 3.049552406711 | -7.3e-11 meV |
| (1,1,0) | 3.049552076126 | -9.7e-11 meV |
| (1,0,1) | 3.049506866524 | -1.0e-10 meV |
| (0.3,0.5,0.8) | 3.049492949565 | -2.4e-11 meV |

`E(n) - E(z)` = 1.235087, 1.230589, 0.615481, 0.426131 meV in both arms, largest
difference **5.4e-11 meV**.

**Relaxed route, not verified, refusal restored.** A harness (`aniso-relaxed2.log`, one
SCF per direction and arm at `conv_thr = 1e-10`) had the totals agreeing to one ulp along
`z` and `x` (-1.9e-10 meV and 0), `K = 0.422516` meV in both. But `z` is the input's own
axis, and along `x` the turn may keep the wedge, so neither exercises a rebuilt wedge
that differs. The committed check at `(1,0,1)` failed on D22 (`aniso-test.log`,
`w-inc-wedge-v7`): wedge -74.40586326777621 Ry against `nosym` -74.40581344308166 Ry,
**0.678 meV** apart, with neither SCF reaching `conv_thr = 1e-10` in 100 iterations
(accuracy 5.5e-10 and 7.0e-9 Ry). That 0.678 meV is the figure of the `(1,0,1)`
direction run on the `z` wedge below, which says the comparison is not yet telling
the rebuild from its absence. So `run_relaxed_direction` refuses a run without `nosym`
again, with that measurement in its message, and the test is
`::test_a_relaxed_direction_with_symmetry_on_is_still_refused`. What lifting it needs: the
`(1,0,1)` pair converged (more `electron_maxstep` or a smaller `mixing_beta`) and then
compared, with the `z`-wedge arm beside it so the alternative reads differently.

**What would have read differently.** The relaxed direction `(1,0,1)` run on the `z`
direction's wedge instead of its own, which is what dropping the refusal without
rebuilding the k-set would do, is **0.678 meV** off. The force theorem on the `z` wedge
is *not* off (1e-10 meV for every direction), and that is exact rather than luck: the
sum over the `z` wedge of a band energy at direction `n` is the average of the whole-grid
band energies at `g^-1 n` over the `z` group, and each of those equals the one at `n`
because `g` is a symmetry of the crystal and its frozen density. So for the force theorem
the old refusal protected nothing on a closed grid; the rebuild is kept because it is
what the relaxed route would need and because it costs the same.

Check: `tests/regression/test_anisotropy.py::test_a_direction_off_the_system_s_own_runs_on_its_own_wedge`
(slow); passed on D22 (`aniso-test.log`).

## What was missing, as found

The entry said "the magnetic group of each direction; the two directions are sampled on
different wedges". The second half is what the refusal claimed and is not a defect: a
band energy and a total energy are scalars, and a scalar's wedge sum is the whole grid's
whenever the wedge is cut by a symmetry of that Hamiltonian from a grid the group maps
onto itself. What was missing is only the rebuild:
`workflows/anisotropy.py:_turned_on_its_own_wedge` turns the texture, rebuilds the k-set
with the turned texture's magnetic group (`System._respin_kpoints`), and checks it tiles
its grid (`system/wedge.py:wedge_group(..., scalar=True)`, which accepts a magnetic group
for a scalar). It is used by `run_force_theorem` (and so `run_anisotropy`) when
`nosym` is off. `run_relaxed_direction` keeps its refusal (above).

Still refused:

- a **projected** force-theorem decomposition off the axis on a wedge: the group permutes
  its site and orbital labels;
- **`run_torque` and `frozen_expectation`** (`_require_nosym_to_turn`, unchanged): they
  differentiate the wedge sum in the direction, and `dH/dtheta` is an axial vector the
  direction's group does not leave alone. The existing test records that on the two
  cobalt cells this tree has, the reduced wedge happens to agree (1e-6 meV), so lifting it
  needs a magnetic group the axial perturbation breaks, which no committed cell has;
- a turned direction whose own wedge does not tile the grid (a shifted grid), with the
  old advice to use `nosym`.
