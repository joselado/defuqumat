The relaxed anisotropy is `E(x) - E(z) = 3.1054e-5` Ry. The by-hand minimum of
`K1 sin^2 + K2 sin^4 - B M sin(theta)` satisfies `kappa = B M / (2 sin theta) = K1 + 2 K2 sin^2 theta`.
The two fields read `kappa = 3.0548e-5` at `sin^2 = 0.1918` and `3.1319e-5` at `sin^2 = 0.6826`, so
`2 K2 = 1.571e-6`, `K1 = 3.0247e-5` and **`K1 + K2 = 3.1032e-5` Ry against the relaxed `3.1054e-5`,
-0.07 per cent**. The convergence parameter is the field, through the angle it holds: one field alone
reads the relaxed anisotropy off by `2 K2 (sin^2 - 1/2)`, measured -1.63 per cent at 26 degrees and
+0.85 per cent at 56, against -1.56 and +0.92 predicted from that `K2`; the two-field extrapolation
removes the `K2` term and leaves `K3`, the next harmonic, which the model does not carry. The slow
test asserts the angles to 0.01 degree, the relaxed anisotropy to 1e-3 relative and `K1 + K2` to it at
5e-3, set from the 0.07 per cent with the `K3` term unknown.

**The alternative reads differently.** With the field's torque left in what the step acts on (the
code before this change, with the refusal bypassed), the run went to the easy axis and stayed there,
0.038 degrees from `c` after 150 iterations and not converged, with the energy of the run along `z`
to 7.7e-9 Ry: the step balanced the anisotropy alone while the electrons, through the soft mode,
pulled towards 55.7 degrees. **The drift pair** says the 55.7116-degree state is a fixed point of the
loop with nothing turning it: a plain SCF from its density under the same field converged in 6
iterations and moved 8e-6 degrees. That plain run also checks the torque formula itself: a run that
does not turn its moments records the full torque, field included, and it read -2.915e-5 Ry/rad
along `y`, where `B M cos(theta)` is 2.92e-5.

**What was missing, and where the entry was right.** The entry said "the Zeeman torque in the step".
That is the term, with one twist the code made visible. The torque recorded each iteration was
`integral of m_out x B[v_scf]`, and `v_scf` holds the applied field (`Calculation.potential` adds it,
as `add_bfield` does inside `v_of_rho`). The output states are stationary in the input Hamiltonian, so
that integral is minus the coupling's torque, the gradient of the anisotropy energy *without* the
Zeeman term. The functional with the field has the gradient that integral plus the Zeeman term's own
derivative, `integral of m_out x b_ext` with the opposite sign, so the two field terms cancel and what
the step must act on is the exchange-correlation field's torque alone. `_zeeman_torque` computes the
field's share from its own potential and the loop subtracts it under `rotate_moments`
(`defuqumat/scf/driver.py`, the `record_orientation` block). What `orientation_torque` records under
`rotate_moments` is that same number, so the stop reads it; a run that does not turn its moments keeps
the field's share, as before. A penalty constraint is physics rather than a missing term, as the entry
says: its fixed point is a stationary point of a modified functional and the constraint holds the
orientation the step moves.

**What stays refused**, each with its test in `tests/unit/test_rotating_moments.py`
(`test_the_sibling_refusals_of_rotate_moments_still_fire_on_the_turning_group`):

- any `constrained_magnetization` (`atomic`, `atomic direction`, `total`, `total direction`,
  `atomic texture`, `fsm`, `atomic fsm`): the constraint holds the orientation;
- `reducebf != 1`: the field, and so the minimum, changes with the iteration count;
- a field without `nosym`, on the turning group `{E, I}`: unchecked with a field, and a per-atom field
  on two atoms related by the inversion breaks it. The conservative choice; whether to admit a uniform
  field on the turning group is the user's open question.
