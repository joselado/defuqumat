Read in words:

* **Against `SCFResult.potential`**, components 1 to 3, which are `B_xc` alone on this cell (`vltot` sits
  in the charge component only and there is no field): the gap is round-off at every `conv_thr`, at most
  5.2e-18 Ry, relative 5e-12. That is because `run_scf` rebuilds the potential at convergence on the output
  `(rho_out, tau_out)` (the `vnew` block, `driver.py` just after `if converged:`), the same pair
  `SCFResult.density` and `SCFResult.tau` hold, so this is an identity of the plumbing and not a
  convergence: it says the torque is built from the run's own `tau`, with the run's own
  `noncollinear_gga`, axis, core charge and spiral argument. The alternative reads differently: dropping
  `tau`'s vector part moves the site torques by 3.7e-6 to 1.2e-4 Ry and replacing `tau` by Thomas-Fermi of
  the charge by 4.0e-6 to 1.2e-4, twelve orders above the agreement.
* **Against the field the last iteration applied**, `potential - potential_change`, which is
  `B_xc[rho_in, tau_in]`: the gap is the self-consistency residual and falls from 2.0e-5 (1e-6) to 7.1e-7
  Ry (1e-12), not monotonically (3.3e-5 at 1e-8), as the field residual falls from 1.5e-2 to 2.0e-5 Ry.
  This is the comparison whose tolerance comes from a convergence; the slow test holds it at 2e-5 at
  `conv_thr = 1e-10` (measured 7.7e-6).

**The site torques of this cell are a residue, and that is the caveat on the whole check.** The 90-degree
spiral has a spin space group that includes inversion through a site combined with a two-fold spin
rotation about that site's moment, which maps site 2 to site 4 and keeps site 3, and turns the
component of a site torque normal to the spin plane into its negative, so that component is zero on every
site at convergence. That component is the one the reading carries, and the largest site torque falls
with `conv_thr` from 2.9e-5 to 9.6e-7 Ry as the moments settle (the transverse moment components go from
0.017 to 0.0005 mu_B). So the check shows that the torque is of the right field; it does not show a
nonzero physical torque on a converged state. The nonzero, `tau`-dependent torque is shown on made-up
fields in the fast test: under `'grad_m'` a total of 0.21 Ry, moved to 0.032 Ry by removing the part of
`tau`'s vector off `m`; under `'kubler'` 1e-17, the null.

A texture where symmetry allows a site torque at convergence would make this a physical number: the
constrained `h2-texture-120.in` (120 degrees, `constrained_magnetization = 'atomic'`) is the cheapest one,
with the constraint's potential subtracted from `SCFResult.potential` for the reference. It was not run,
since the three D22 runs of the stop rule were used (run 1 below did not converge, run 2 found the
identity).

**The runs.** Run 1 (`harness1.log`, `w-inc-I110`): the cell as committed, 12 bohr across the chain,
did not converge in 300 iterations at `conv_thr = 1e-6` (580 s), with `max |B_rebuilt - B_scf| = 18 Ry`,
which is TB09's potential in the vacuum; killed. Run 2 (`harness2-a5.log`, `harness2-a65.log`,
`w-inc-I110-v2`): at 5 bohr it converged at every threshold and showed the identity; at 6.5 bohr it did
not converge in 300 iterations (60 Ry field residual) and was stopped. Run 3 is the table.

**What was missing, and where the entry was wrong.** The entry said "`tau` from the states rather than
the density (sweep-core)". Nothing had to be computed: `SCFResult.tau` already holds the converged,
mixed kinetic energy density, the partner of `SCFResult.density`, and `torque_of_result` passed a literal
`None` in `v_of_rho`'s `tau` slot. The fix is the argument: `torque_of_result(calculation, result,
density=None, tau=None)` reads `result.tau` when `density` is the run's own. Two refusals stay:

* a caller's `density` with no `tau`: `tau`'s vector part turns with the spins, so the converged `tau`
  beside a turned magnetization would be the field of a state that does not exist. Whether such a call
  should default to `result.tau` instead is the user's decision; it is kept refused
  (`tests/unit/test_spin_torque_meta.py::test_a_density_without_its_tau_is_refused`);
* a result with `tau = None` (`::test_a_result_without_tau_is_refused`).

A sibling unaffected by this: the `nspin_mag = 1` spinor run is refused by `exchange_torque`'s own shape
check. A PAW meta-GGA run gets the grid field only, as a PAW GGA run already does. And since TB09 has no
energy, the module docstring's argument for a zero *total* torque without spin-orbit coupling does not
apply to it; on this cell the total is 9.9e-10 Ry at 1e-12, falling with `conv_thr`, so the spiral's
symmetry zeroes it here.

**Docstrings changed beside the fix.** `spin_torque.py`'s module docstring and
`Calculator.get_exchange_torque` said the torque is identically zero unless `nosource`; that was already
untrue under `noncollinear_gga = 'grad_m'` for a GGA, and is untrue for the meta case now. Both name
`'grad_m'` as the third case.

**The user guide.** `docs/guide/magnetism.md`'s "Limits" note does not mention this refusal, so nothing
is deleted there. The section "The source-free exchange-correlation field" has a sentence that is wrong
under `'grad_m'`, for a GGA and now for a meta-GGA:

> Its `sites` hold one torque vector per atom, and its `parallel_fraction` is 1 without `nosource`, so
> that a zero torque can be told from a field that is parallel to the moment by construction.

Replacement:

> Its `sites` hold one torque vector per atom. Its `parallel_fraction` is 1 when the field is parallel to
> the moment by construction. This is the case for a local functional, and for a gradient or meta-GGA
> functional with `noncollinear_gga = 'kubler'`. So a zero torque can be told from a field that cannot
> turn the moment. With `nosource`, or with `noncollinear_gga = 'grad_m'`, the field has a part across
> the moment and the torque can be nonzero. A meta-GGA such as TB09 needs the kinetic energy density of
> the states, and the torque takes it from the converged run.

And one sentence for the "Limits" note:

> `get_exchange_torque()` under a meta-GGA uses the kinetic energy density of the converged run. A call
> of the torque at another density must pass the kinetic energy density that belongs to it.
