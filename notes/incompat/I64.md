# I64. Real-time propagation x a ground state under a magnetic field or constrained moment

Status: done for the frozen potential with any field or constraint, and for the updated
potentials (`'hartree'`, `'hxc'`) with an applied, `reducebf`-scaled or feedback (`fsm`)
field. A **penalty** constraint (`'atomic'`, `'atomic direction'`, `'atomic texture'`,
`'total'`, `'total direction'`) with the potential updated is refused by name, by the
conservative rule: whether the dynamics should carry the penalty is a decision for the
user, and it is recorded below as an open question.

## The number

Silicon, two atoms, LDA, `ecutwfc = 12`, `nspin = 2`, `tot_magnetization = 0`,
`occupations = 'fixed'`, `nosym`, 2x2x2 unshifted, a `LOCAL_MAGNETIC_FIELDS` field of
0.05 Ry along z on the first atom's sphere only, `reducebf = 0.8`, SCF `conv_thr = 1e-11`
(28 iterations, converged `field_scale = 2.418e-3`). Then 200 steps of `dt = 0.1` a.u. at
zero amplitude (`Kick(0.0)`). One run on D22, CPU, one core, 77 s
(`/l/ladovj1/review/incompat/I64/harness1.log`, commit `26a4ad6`).

| route | `excited` (electrons out of the carried subspace) | band-energy spread (Ha) |
|---|---|---|
| `run_realtime`, `potential = 'frozen'`, converged pair | **4.31e-10** | 3.7e-11 |
| `run_realtime`, `potential = 'hxc'`, converged pair | **4.31e-10** | 3.7e-11 |
| the SCF's own occupied states, `v_scf` from the converged pair | **4.97e-10** | 3.7e-11 |
| the SCF's own occupied states, `v_scf` from the input field (what the workflow froze before) | **8.93e-3** | 1.45e-7 |

The current moves by 3.3e-11 over the run on both workflow routes. The identity is that
eigenstates of a frozen Hamiltonian are stationary at zero field, which shares no
machinery with the field bookkeeping.

**The alternative hypothesis reads differently, and that is why the cell is what it is.**
Through the workflow the check is stationary whatever field is handed in, because the
fixed-density solve and the frozen potential then use the same field; and a *uniform*
collinear Zeeman field, right or wrong, is a rigid shift of each channel and leaves every
eigenstate an eigenstate. So the A/B is on the SCF's own states (solved under the
converged field) and on a field that varies in space, where the input-field potential
puts 8.9e-3 electrons out of the occupied subspace in 20 a.u., seven orders above the
floor. A collinear `fsm` run (a uniform converged field) cannot be told apart by this
check, for the same reason.

## What was missing

The entry says the frozen potential is rebuilt from the input field. That is right, but
the refusal that named it was not the one a user met:

- `workflows/realtime.py:_solved_states` called `fixed_density_states` with no `field` or
  `field_scale`, so through `Calculator.get_realtime` and every `run_*` the error was the
  fixed-density solve's `ValueError` ("needs the field the SCF ended with"), raised before
  `require_a_realtime_regime` ran. The driver's `NotImplementedError` spoke only for a
  `Calculation` handed to `propagate` directly, which never rebuilds the field at all: it
  freezes the `v_scf` the caller passes. The driver's guard was inherited from the response
  stack's (`response/sternheimer.py`), where the stack does rebuild the potential; here it
  belonged to the workflow (trap: inherit a refusal only after checking which machine it
  belongs to).
- The term is the pair `SCFResult.magnetic_field` and `.field_scale` threaded into the
  solve and into the frozen potential, `calc.potential(density, field_scale, field)`, with
  `is None` and not `or` (a fully decayed field has `field_scale = 0.0`). All six entry
  points (`run_realtime`, `run_realtime_dielectric`, `run_hhg`, `run_harmonic_orders`,
  `run_third_harmonic`, `run_nonlinear_spectrum`) take `field=` and `field_scale=`, and
  `Calculator._call_options` fills them from `_STATE_ARGUMENTS` with no edit to the facade.
- The updated potential, which the entry does not mention. `_Run` built `U[rho]` from
  `calculation.potential(rho)`, the input field at full scale. In `v_scf + U[rho(t)] - U[rho0]`
  an applied field cancels, its energy being linear in m, but `E_U` is `ehart + etxc`, so
  the energy the run conserves would have been one `int v_field rho` away from the
  potential it propagates in (trap: the energy right while its derivative is wrong).
  `U` now takes `field_scale = 0`, the applied field stays frozen in `v_scf` at its
  converged strength, and potential and energy agree. A penalty constraint is the one term
  that moves with `rho(t)`; it is refused in `require_a_potential_mode`, which every
  updated route (real time, orders, the self-consistent hierarchy) calls. The `jvp` kernels
  in `hierarchy.py` and `dense.py` see no applied field, its derivative being exactly zero.
- Not measured: a spinor (`nspin = 4`) ground state under a field at an updated potential.
  The argument above covers it and the collinear number does not exercise the energy
  term, since the per-channel count is conserved there.

The question left for the user: should the updated potential carry a penalty
constraint's curvature (the functional the ground state minimised) or freeze the
converged constraint field (an applied field at the converged strength)? The second is
what `'frozen'` does now; the first puts `lambda`, a convergence parameter, into the
response.

A stale sentence noticed, not changed: the warning in `scf/driver.py`
(`_warn_if_the_field_did_not_fade`) ends "every response entry point refuses such a
state by name". The real-time entry points no longer do; they take the pair.

## The user guide and the README

`docs/guide/nonlinear.md`, the "Limits" note, the sentence

> The real-time methods give an error for DFT+U, a spin spiral, a ground state
> under a magnetic field or with a constrained moment, a static electric field
> (`tefield` or `lelfield`), and a symmetry-reduced k-set passed as `kpoints`
> (you pass `grid=` instead, which builds the right reduction for the field).

becomes

> The real-time methods give an error for DFT+U, a spin spiral, a static electric field
> (`tefield` or `lelfield`), and a symmetry-reduced k-set passed as `kpoints`
> (you pass `grid=` instead, which builds the right reduction for the field).
> A ground state under a magnetic field or with a constrained moment works.
> The run uses the field that the SCF ended with, not the field in the input.
> With `potential='hartree'` or `'hxc'`, a penalty constraint (for example
> `constrained_magnetization = 'atomic'`) gives an error. With `potential='frozen'` it works.

README feature table: the row "Real-time response to a laser pulse" (README.md line 706)
names no regime and its `QE`/`Elk` cells do not change. No README edit.
