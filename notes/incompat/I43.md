## What was done

The entry said "Test: none", and that was right: nothing fired
`tddft/spinchi0.py:require_a_transverse_regime`'s wedge clause. I wrote
`tests/unit/test_wedge_refusals.py::test_a_magnon_on_a_symmetry_reduced_k_set_is_refused`,

The guard's message is sharpened to name the term as found in the code.

## What is missing, as found

The entry named two things, and only one of them is the hard one.

- Symmetrising `X_0(G, G')` over the group of `q`, both labels at once. This now exists
  for `chi_0` at `q -> 0` (`tddft/chi0.py:_completed`, I42, measured at 2.1e-10). At
  finite `q` it is the same phased permutation restricted to the little group of `q`,
  so this part is a small extension.
- **The states at `k + q`.** `commensurate_shift` looks `k + q` up in the same k-set. A
  wedge does not hold it, and taking it from its representative `S k'` means **rotating a
  wavefunction**: a permutation of the G sphere, a phase `exp(-i (k+G).t)` and, because
  the run is spin-polarized, the spin part. Nothing in the code rotates a wavefunction
  (I grepped for one; `transport.whole_grid` says "unfolding a wavefunction means
  rotating it" and re-solves instead). This is the term that blocks, and the entry did not
  name it.
- On top of that, the k-set of a magnet is cut with its **magnetic** group, whose `t_rev`
  operations swap the `+-` and `-+` blocks. `system/wedge.py:wedge_group` refuses a
  magnetic group for every response for that reason, and no magnetic cell has been
  measured.

The cheapest route that does not need a wavefunction rotation is to re-solve the
fixed-density bands on `k + q` for the wedge points, which is a second NSCF per `q`; that
is a design choice for the user, not something to do overnight.
