# I90. Piezoelectric tensor x polar crystal: done (norm-conserving); ultrasoft stays refused

**The number.** The cell is `alas-raman.in` (norm-conserving LDA AlAs, 10 Ry, `nosym`) with As
moved from `a(1/4, 1/4, 1/4)` to `a(1/4, 1/4, 0.29)`. Its point group is `mm2`, polar along `z`,
and its spontaneous polarization relative to zincblende on one branch is
`P = (1.6e-4, -1.6e-4, -0.17962) C/m^2` (the `x`, `y` parts are the Berry side's floor). The quantum
is 1.447 C/m^2 per direction. The raw branch the phase folding lands on is
`(0.513, -0.513, -0.693)`, which is what an improper tensor would carry.

The reference is a Berry-phase finite difference with no response machinery in it, Elk's route
(`piezoelt.f90`). Each strain gets its own ground state on a 6^3 mesh at `conv_thr = 1e-10`. Each
ground state gets three string sets (`nppstr = 11` over 6x6), and the wrapped phase differences are
contracted with the **unstrained** lattice vectors and volume. That contraction is the proper tensor.
The harness is `notes/incompat/I90-harness/harness.py`, and it ran on D22, CPU, one core, in one run
of 1484 s (`/l/ladovj1/review/incompat/I90/run1.log` and `out1/result.json`, worktree
`/l/ladovj1/w-inc-I90`). The response route is `method = 'zstar_eu'` on the whole unshifted grid, with
the refusal patched out in the harness. Every number below is one sample, in C/m^2.

The distance of the response route from the proper finite difference (the mean of the two steps
where two were taken) falls with the k-mesh. For `e31`, `e33`, `e15` and `e14` it is 1.1e-2, 6.6e-2,
6.1e-2 and 1.24e-1 at 4^3, then 3.5e-3, 1.1e-2, 1.2e-2 and 3.0e-2 at 6^3, then **2.8e-3, 5.4e-4,
1.6e-3** and 1.17e-2 at 8^3. That is the same geometric approach the zincblende calibration showed (15,
3.7 and 0.9 per cent at 4, 6 and 8). What is left of `e14`, 1.7 per cent, is the 10 Ry cutoff, which
the finite difference feels and the response does not (1.2 per cent on zincblende, `OPEN.md` Part
XIII item 3). The finite difference has its own floor of about 5e-3: its forbidden `e_(x)zz` reads
5.1e-3, and `e31` moves 2.6e-3 between the two steps, so the 8^3 residuals on `e31`, `e33` and `e15`
are at that floor.

**The control.** Three readings of the contraction are possible, and they differ on exactly the
components a `delta P` term reaches. The improper tensor, `e^proper + sym(delta_ki P_j) - delta_ij
P_k` with the spontaneous `P`, puts `e31` at 0.241 and `e15` at -0.202. The full mixed derivative at
a fixed Cartesian field adds the frozen term `delta_ki P_j`, which puts `e33` at -0.461. At 8^3 the
response route is 0.18, 0.09 and 0.18 from these, against residuals of 3e-3 or less from the proper
form. So the agreement could not have been read with a `delta P` term present. The sign of the
improper relation is fixed by the finite difference's own arithmetic, since the raw-branch improper
column it prints equals the proper column plus `sym(delta_ki P_j) - delta_ij P_k`, with the raw
`P`, to 5.2e-5 C/m^2 at worst over all twelve differences (the residual is of order `s P`, since `P`
is taken at the unstrained cell).

**What was missing, as found in the code: nothing.** The entry said the improper-to-proper
correction `delta_ki P_j - delta_ij P_k` was missing and that the obstacle was the lack of a
polarization. Both halves were wrong. The contraction of the field response against the strain
derivative at frozen plane-wave coefficients is already the proper tensor. Written as the mixed
derivative of `E - Omega E.P`, the frozen-state strain derivative of `Omega P_k` is `delta_ki Omega
P_j`, which is exactly the lattice's part of `d(Omega P_k)/d(eps_ij)`, so the contraction is the
part the reduced phases carry. The module docstring had it backwards: it called the contraction
improper, and it also had the sign of the `delta P` terms reversed. The docstring at
`piezoelectric_zstar_eu_style` said the frozen term "vanishes for the non-polar crystals this is
allowed on", which was true and beside the point. Both are rewritten. Nothing is added to the
assembly. `require_a_nonpolar_crystal` is deleted rather than left as a no-op, and
`require_a_measured_polar_crystal` takes its place in the chain.

The proper tensor is also the only one that does not depend on the branch of `P`. The improper one
moves by a quantum, 1.45 C/m^2 on this cell, with the branch. That is a second argument against
ever building the correction from `P`.

**What stays refused.** A polar crystal with an **ultrasoft** dataset. The argument above needs the
reduced phases at frozen coefficients to be strain-independent. That holds for `sum_G c* c` over the
integer sphere, but not when `S` is in the Berry overlaps, because its `vkb` is `beta(|k+G|)` and
deforms with the cell. That term has been checked only on zincblende `e_14`, where every polar
component is zero by symmetry. PAW is refused earlier, by `require_a_measured_dataset`, as before.
Lifting the ultrasoft half would take the same harness on a displaced `alas-piezo.in` (25 Ry, a
much slower cell), which this unit did not run.

  replaces `test_a_polar_crystal_is_refused_by_name`. The harness ran the same cell, mesh, method and
  `conv_thr`, and its 8^3 column is the evidence that the assertion holds. The raw numbers are
  committed as `notes/incompat/I90-harness/result.json`.

**User guide**, `docs/guide/piezo.md`, the "Limits" note. Replace

> A polar crystal, one whose crystal class leaves a direction invariant, gives an error, because
> there the measured tensor differs from the derivative by terms built from the spontaneous
> polarization. A PAW dataset gives an error, while norm-conserving and ultrasoft datasets work on

with

> The code computes the proper piezoelectric tensor. This is the one an experiment measures, and it
> does not depend on the branch of the polarization. So a polar crystal works with a
> norm-conserving dataset. We checked this on AlAs with one atom moved along $z$, against a
> Berry-phase finite difference. A polar crystal with an ultrasoft dataset gives an error, because
> there the projectors in the Berry overlaps change with the strain, and we have not checked that
> term. A PAW dataset gives an error. Norm-conserving and ultrasoft datasets work on any k-mesh for
> a crystal that is not polar.
