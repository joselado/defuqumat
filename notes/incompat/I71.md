# I71. Residual (Newton) solver x tetrahedra or `from_input` occupations: done

**The numbers.** Two direct `pw.x` references, measured on D22 (worktree `w-inc-I71`, harness
`review/incompat/I71/harness.py`, log `harness1.log`, one sample each, single core):

- Bloechl tetrahedra, `tests/data/qe/al-tetrahedra.in` (fcc Al, 4x4x4 shifted grid, 10 k-points).
  `scf_solver='newton-krylov'` (`forcing=0.5`, `warmup=3`, `conv_thr=1e-10`) gives -4.1978985974 Ry,
  **2.6e-9 Ry** from `pw.x` 7.5's -4.19789860 (`tests/data/qe/reference.out.al-tetrahedra:202`), and
  6.1e-10 Ry from the mixing loop on the same input. The Fermi level is 3.0e-7 Ry from the mixing loop's,
  and the density 4.8e-8 at most (the density peaks at 3.4e-2). 18 Newton steps, 6 Jacobian actions, 1.2 s,
  against 5 mixing iterations in 2.3 s.
- `from_input`, `pw_scf/scf-occ.in` (Si, 2 k-points, an `OCCUPATIONS` card). Newton gives
  -15.7944955709 Ry, **8.8e-10 Ry** from `pw.x`'s -15.79449557
  (`tests/data/qe/reference.out.pw_scf-scf-occ:185`), and 3.8e-13 Ry from the mixing loop. Density
  4.0e-8 apart at most.

The autodiff Jacobian on the tetrahedron cell (`harness2.py`, logs `harness2.log`, `harness2raw.log`), at
the converged state, warm, measured on `dF v = J v + v`, whose size is 2.2e-3 to 2.4e-3 on a unit random
direction (the occupations' part, which `|J v|`, about 1, hides): the autodiff action is 2.6e-2, 1.1e-2
and 2.3e-2 of `|dF v|` from the central difference on three random directions (seeds 0, 1, 2), and 7.1e-2
on a uniform direction. With the custom tangent removed (the bare bisection), the same three read 1.22,
0.045 and 1.38, and the uniform one 0.10. So the implicit tangent is what makes the action right on two of
three random directions, and the 1 to 7 per cent left is the size the warm-start map's derivative is
known to differ from the autodiff one by (`residual.py` docstring: 1.5e-3 on silicon to 5e-2 and more on
other cells), which I did not separate further. The slow test's own form, with the guard-fires half in the same process
(`harness3.py`, worktree `w-inc-I71-v2`, `harness3.log`), reads 2.59e-2 against its bound 0.1 and 1.22 with
the bare bisection patched in, against its floor 0.5.

**What was missing, and where the entry was wrong.** The entry said "differentiable tetrahedron weights
(sweep-core)". That was not missing: `scf/tetrahedra.py` was already JAX end to end on the eigenvalues
(`tetrahedron_weights_at` and `tetrahedron_occupations` are jitted, the DOS is `jax.grad` of
`_occupied_fraction`, and the dead branches are clamped for exactly that reason). The tetrahedra
themselves are host-built, but from the k-grid and the group, not from the density, so they are a constant
of `F` like `wk`. And `from_input` being "not a function of the eigenvalues at all" makes it a constant,
whose zero tangent is the right derivative. What was missing was:

1. The two dispatch branches in `scf/residual.py:_weights`, mirroring `Calculation.occupations`. The
   tetrahedron table is now built by one accessor, `Calculation.tetrahedron_table()`
   (`scf/driver.py`), used by both, so the two cannot build different tables.
2. A tangent for the tetrahedron Fermi level, which only `jvp='autodiff'` reads. `_bisect` is a
   `fori_loop` whose tangent is a mixture of the bracket ends' (`min e`, `max e`) and is wrong without
   failing. `scf/tetrahedra.py:_fermi_level` now carries a `custom_jvp` with the implicit derivative of
   `N(E_F) = nelec`, both pieces taken from `integrated_states` by `jax.jvp`, zero in a gap. The primal
   is the same bisection, so every forward number is unchanged.

The default `jvp='finite-difference'` needed neither the tangent nor anything else beyond (1).

**What stays refused.** `jvp='sternheimer'` with tetrahedra (needs `dfpt_tetra_beta`) and with
`from_input` (`require_a_sternheimer_regime`), each with a test,
`tests/regression/test_scf_solvers.py::test_the_sternheimer_jacobian_still_refuses_these_occupations`.
The magnetic-field and spiral refusals of `make_residual` are untouched.
