# I20. Force x spin spiral: done (norm-conserving)

**Measured.** On the NiI chain at `q = b3/4` (`tests/data/qe/nii-chain-spiral.in`)
against its four-cell supercell (`nii-chain-4cell.in`, `soc_scale = 0`),
both diagonalised at one density (the spiral's SCF density, re-solved at
`conv_thr = 1e-10` in the unit cell and unfolded onto the supercell), the force
on each of the eight supercell atoms equals the spiral's on its nickel or
iodine to **1.17e-8 Ry/bohr** on forces of 1.23e-2 Ry/bohr (the iodine is
off the chain, so the force is not a zero of symmetry); the uncorrected net
force agrees to 1.9e-8 per cell. One D22 run, default `noncollinear_gga =
'grad_m'` (`review/incompat/spiral/v3-nii.log`). The functional reproduces
the spiral SCF total energy to **1.1e-13 Ry**. Test:
`tests/regression/test_spiral_supercell.py::test_the_spiral_force_is_the_supercell_force`
and `::test_the_functional_is_the_spiral_total_energy` (slow).

**What was missing.** The entry's term was right in substance and wrong in
size: `forces/spiral.py` already wrote the pair contraction for `dE/dq`, and
`at_positions` already rebuilt `vkb` on the doubled `2 nk` list. The only gap
was dispatch: `energy_at` and `forces/chunked._separable` sent every
`noncolin` run to `_spinor_projector_energies`, which projected both
components on one `vkb`. It now takes `spiral=True` and stacks
`vkb[:nk]`, `vkb[nk:]` on the spinor axis. Two further things surfaced:

- **A crash, not a refusal, on the default functional.** A `'grad_m'` spiral
  force traced on a cold cache died in `spiral_box_frequencies` with
  `TracerArrayConversionError`, because `cell.bg` is a JAX computation and
  returns a tracer inside a trace even of a captured concrete cell. It now
  builds `bg` from `cell.at` on the host (`scf/potential.py`). The force test
  uses a fresh `Calculation` so this path is the one exercised.
- **An SCF floor that is larger than the identity.** The same spiral's force
  from the SCF's own states and from states re-solved at the density the SCF
  reports differ by **1.8e-4 Ry/bohr** (1.4 per cent) at the input's
  `conv_thr = 1e-9`; under `'kubler'` the supercell at the reported density
  differed from the SCF states by energy terms of 1e-4 to 1e-3 Ry with the
  total equal to 1.6e-7, and a Fermi level 1.8e-6 Ry apart. That is why the
  identity is taken at one input density. Whether it is spiral-specific was
  not measured; it reads like the magnetization part of the convergence
  measure being weak, and is a candidate for `OPEN.md`.

**Still refused** (`forces/energy.py:reject_spinor_spiral`): an ultrasoft or PAW
spiral (every term is in the functional, but the only augmented spiral cells
have one atom each, so a force check is a zero), and a spiral with DFT+U (the
projectors move with the atoms; unchecked). The analytic force refuses spinors
as before.

**User guide**, `docs/guide/forces.md`, Limits. Replace

> `get_forces()` gives an error for a spin spiral, because its two spinor
> components live on different plane-wave spheres and the nonlocal term then
> needs the projectors of both. So `get_relax()` gives an error for a spiral
> too.

with

> `get_forces()` works for a norm-conserving spin spiral, where each spinor
> component meets the projectors of its own sphere. We checked it against the
> four-cell supercell of the NiI chain, to 1e-8 Ry/bohr. It gives an error for
> an ultrasoft or PAW spiral and for a spiral with DFT+U, which have no check.

(`get_relax()` on a spiral was not run; leave it out of the replacement or
state that it is untested.) `docs/guide/spirals.md`, Limits: in "A spin spiral
has no force on the atoms, no stress, no site angular momenta, no orbital
magnetization and no magnons in the code." remove "no force on the atoms,"
(see I21, I24 and I23 for the rest of that sentence).
