# I31. Ultracell x gradient-corrected functional

Status: partial. A gradient-corrected functional now runs on the ultracell at `nspin = 1`, and
is refused by name at `nspin = 2` and `nspin = 4`.

## The number

`tests/regression/test_ultracell_regimes.py::test_a_gga_ultracell_bounds_the_gga_supercell_from_above`,
PBE silicon (`Si.pbe-rrkj.UPF`), a 2x1x1 ultracell on the `(1, 2, 2)` mesh of `k0`, against the
explicit 2x1x1 PBE supercell SCF of the same modulation at `conv_thr = 1e-11` (the check of the
entry, exact by the nested basis of P88). Two D22 runs, CPU, one core:

| run | commit (worktree) | tiled null | energy above the supercell, per cell, at nbnd = 12, 24, 48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `r1-gga.log` | `ec98a1f` (`w-inc-ultracell`) | dV 5.55e-16, density 1.11e-7, energy -2.31e-14 Ry | +9.100e-5, +3.529e-6, +4.144e-7 Ry |
| `r6-gga.log` | `dc0d29a` (`w-inc-ultracell-v5`) | the same | the same, to the four digits printed |

So the ultracell energy lies above the supercell's at every truncation and falls monotonically
to 4.1e-7 Ry per cell at 48 bands, meaning that the box gradient is the supercell's gradient:
a gradient taken cell by cell, as Elk's `potxcu` takes it, would give a different functional,
and the variational bound would then not be a statement about it. The second run was taken
because `potential.py` changed between the two commits (the meta-GGA commit `b89dbff`); the
commits after `dc0d29a` change only the guard. Single runs, not medians; the figure is an
energy difference of a deterministic loop, and the two runs agree.

## The polarized case, refused

`test_a_polarized_gga_ultracell_converges_to_the_supercell` (PBE by `input_dft` on the partly
polarized hydrogen lattice, 2x1x1 on `(2, 2, 2)`) did not close in the three runs the stop rule
allows:

- `r1-gga2.log` (`ec98a1f`): the unit-cell SCF stalled at 8.9e-10 Ry after 100 iterations
  against `conv_thr = 1e-11`.
- `r3-gga2.log` (`w-inc-ultracell-v3`): with the SCF stopped at 1e-9, the tiled null left the
  density 1.17e-4 of its maximum from the frozen states' own, against a 1e-5 tolerance.
- `r5-gga2.log` (`dc0d29a`): with the null tolerance at 1e-3 (null energy -4.13e-9 Ry, dV
  4.55e-15) and the supercell mixed at 0.3, the ultracell loop at `nbnd = 8` stalled at
  dr2 = 2.392e-5 Ry (charge 1.532e-5, magnetization 8.603e-6) after 200 iterations against
  `conv_thr = 1e-8`, with a multiplet gap of 4.78e-10 Ry at the truncation.

So `require_an_ultracell_functional` (`defuqumat/ultracell/potential.py`) now refuses a GGA at
`nspin_mag = 2` with those numbers in its message, and the polarized test is a strict `xfail`
on that `NotImplementedError`, to be lifted with the guard. The `nspin_mag = 4` refusal (the
local spin frame of the noncollinear GGA, not measured) is unchanged. What is open is whether
the stall is the soft moment of this cell (an SCF stop that cannot reach 1e-11 points that way)
or the box gradient at `nspin = 2`; a stiffer polarized cell is what tells the two apart.

## What was missing

The entry names the gradient of the ultracell density including the envelope's own gradient at
order `Q`. That is right in substance, and what it took in the code was not a correction term
added to a cell-periodic gradient: the ultracell potential is now `v_of_rho` itself, run on the
box's own reciprocal vectors (the tiled unit-cell dense sphere written as a `GVectors` on the
supercell lattice), so the gradient is taken over the whole modulation exactly as the supercell
takes it. The LDA path reproduces the old one to 4e-16 Ry on silicon, as `ec98a1f`'s message reports
(not rerun here); the LDA ultracell file `tests/regression/test_ultracell.py` passed whole on
D22 at that commit (`r1-old.log`, 34 passed).

## User guide

`docs/guide/ultracell.md`, the Limits note, the paragraph that starts "The functional must be
LDA." Replace only its first two sentences,

> The functional must be LDA. A gradient-corrected functional gives an error,
> because the gradient of the density then contains the gradient of the
> envelope, and a meta-GGA also gives an error.

with

> The functional must be LDA, or a gradient-corrected functional on a cell with no
> spin polarization. The code takes the gradient over the whole long cell, so it
> contains the gradient of the envelope. A gradient-corrected functional on a
> spin-polarized or noncollinear cell gives an error, because only the unpolarized
> case has been checked against a supercell. A meta-GGA also gives an error.

The meta-GGA and `nosource` sentences of that paragraph belong to I33 and I34 and are left to
those records.
