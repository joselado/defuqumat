## The feedback field (I73-feedback, 2026-10-17)

The user decided on 2026-10-16 that the Newton-Krylov solver carries the feedback field of a
fixed-spin-moment constraint in its solved state, as it carries the DFT+U `ns`. Branch
`incompat/I73-feedback` from `incompat-t2` at `e39c74f7`.

**What the fixed point is.** `run_scf` moves the field once an iteration, after the mix, by Elk's rule
`B <- B - tau (m(rho_out) - m_fix)` (`MagneticField.feedback`, `fsm_update = 'elk'`, the default), with
`tau = lambda` the gain and the moment read off the *output* density. Its fixed point is the pair
(`rho`, `B`) at which the density is self-consistent under `B` and the moment of that density is the
target. The residual now carries the driven leaf (`uniform` for `fsm`, `atomic` for the two per-atom
schemes) as the last block of `x`, and its row of `F` is that same rule as a function of the state
(`MagneticField.elk_driven`, with the error orthogonalised against the targets for `atomic fsm
direction`, exactly `_atomic_step`'s Elk branch). So the field rows of the residual are
`r_B = -tau (m - m_fix)`. The two candidates the task named, `m - m_fix` and the field update itself,
have the same root and differ by the factor `tau`, and the update is the one taken: `tau` is Elk's
estimate of `1/chi` (0.02 against the measured 0.022 on `fe-fsm.in`, `fields.py` `FSM_UPDATES`), so the
field block of the Jacobian, `-tau dm/dB`, sits near `-1`, the scale of the density block, and the
identity that `kerker_preconditioner` applies past the density is the right preconditioner for it. For
`atomic fsm direction` the rows along each target are identically zero; the Krylov vectors then never
move the parallel part of `B`, which the loop never moves either.

`unpack` keeps its four-tuple and the field block is read by `ScfResidual.unpack_field`; it is last in
the vector, so every other offset is unchanged. `run_scf` passes its own `field` (after
`_carried_driven_field`, so a field carried from a starting state is where Newton starts) and takes the
solved field back from `NewtonKrylovResult.field` before the loop, so the hand-over loop continues from
it rather than driving the field again from the input.

**The convergence measure (changed 2026-10-17, the user's decision).** `accuracy_of(r, x)` adds
`ScfResidual.field_accuracy(r, x) = |dm| (|B| + tau |dm|)`, with `B` the field the state `x` holds,
`dm = m - m_fix` read off the field rows `r_B = -tau dm`, and both norms over the whole driven leaf. The
reason is the raw total. It leaves out the Zeeman term, and along the constrained family `dE/dm = B`, so a
state that stopped `dm` off its target reads `B . dm` off in the total, first order, and `|B . dm| <= |B|
|dm|`: the first term bounds exactly that error in the quantity `conv_thr` is a statement about. The
second term, `tau |dm|^2`, is the measure of the first round, kept on purpose: `|B| |dm|` alone is zero at
`B = 0` for any `dm`, so a state converged in the density at no field would pass it, and the sum is `|dm|`
times `|B| + |B - B'|` with `B' = B - tau dm` Elk's next field, so it bounds `|B'| |dm|` and vanishes only
at `dm = 0` (`test_the_field_rows_count_towards_convergence` checks both: 1e-9 mu_B off at `B = -0.011`
Ry is above 1e-11, the same at `B = 0` is below). `newton_krylov` now hands the state to `accuracy_of`
beside the residual. The first round's measure, `tau |dm|^2` alone, bounded the moment to about 2e-5
mu_B at 1e-11 and left the raw total 8e-8 to 6e-7 Ry off the loop (the first table below).

outer steps, Newton on `fe-fsm.in` stopped at accuracy 1.8e-11 with the moment 1.7e-9 mu_B off and handed
over; the loop accepted it in one iteration, `SCFResult.converged` read True, and the raw total was 1.8e-11
Ry off the loop's (`v3-fsm.log`). Two runs started from a state away from the target: from the converged
2.2 mu_B state Newton stopped at 40 steps at 1.1e-10 with the raw total 1.1e-10 Ry off, again reported
converged (`v2-fsm.log`); from the *unconverged* 1.8 mu_B state the Newton field ended 0.58 Ry off and the
loop did the work in 53 iterations, landing 6.5e-7 Ry off the converged loop (`v-fsm.log`). These are the
cases `run_scf`'s hand-over warning (next paragraph) is for.

**The silent hand-over is now a warning.** When Newton stops without converging under a feedback scheme,
`run_scf` warns by name (`driver._warn_unconverged_hand_over`): the accuracy against `conv_thr`, the
number of outer steps, the field share `|dm| (|B| + lambda |dm|)` of the accuracy, which bounds the
first-order error of the total, and the tolerance the loop will accept the field at, `FSM_TOLERANCE` (1e-3
mu_B) or `FSM_ANGLE_TOLERANCE` (0.1 degrees) for `atomic fsm direction`, saying that `SCFResult.converged`
can then read True with the total off by about the share. `NewtonKrylovResult.field_accuracy` carries the
share at the returned state (the driver keeps the last value its `accuracy_of` computed, which
`newton_krylov` calls on accepted states only). Silent when Newton converged or carried no feedback field,
where the loop's own test is the density's, `conv_thr` itself. Tested in
`tests/unit/test_residual_fields.py` (`test_an_unconverged_hand_over_under_a_feedback_field_warns`, for
the three schemes and both tolerances, and its converse, on a stand-in result), and from inside a real
`run_scf` by `test_run_scf_warns_when_newton_hands_over_short_under_a_feedback_field` (the silicon `fsm`
cell, Newton cut to one outer step).

**Why it stopped at 40, and the fix that was not needed.** The 40-step budget, measured: the same runs at
80 steps converge at 63 and 60 (table above), so there was no floor. What the first round of this section
took for a floor was read off a budget that ran out while the accuracy was still wandering. The account
kept for the record: the mechanism suspected was scale, since GMRES stops on a *relative* raw 2-norm and
the backtracking accepts on `||r||`, and in Ry the field rows, `tau dm` = 3e-10 at the stop, are small
beside the density rows. The experiment carried the field as `B / tau` in `x`, so the rows read `-dm` in
mu_B, 50 times heavier, with the Jacobian's field block unchanged (commit `acaab72c`, worktree `-v5`,
`h5-*.log`), and within 40 steps it did not converge either: `atomic fsm` stopped at 8.7e-10, `atomic fsm
direction` at 6.6e-9, and on `fe-fsm.in` it converged at 1e-11 in 37 outer steps. The scaling was
reverted (the branch carries the field in Ry) and the per-step `||r||_2` in the Newton history was kept.
The reading that the density rows sit at a 2-norm floor of eigensolver noise and decide the Newton
direction was an inference from `||r||_2` not falling over the last steps; the 80-step runs show it does
not stop convergence, at most slows it. A weighted inner product for GMRES and the line search, or a Schur
step on the field block, might cut the step count; neither is measured, and neither is needed for
convergence.

**The measure kept is the new one.** The numbers support it: the raw total moved from 8e-8 to 8e-13 Ry on
`fe-fsm.in` and from 5.6e-7 to 7.0e-13 under `atomic fsm` on the canted pair, and `|B| |dm|` is the
quantity `conv_thr` ought to bound. The old measure stopped Newton early by not asking for the field at
all. **The status is done**: with 80 outer steps the raw total agrees with the loop to 8e-13 Ry on
`fe-fsm.in` and 7e-13 under `atomic fsm`, and under `atomic fsm direction` Newton's raw total is the
reference's energy at the target, the 1.0e-9 Ry raw difference being the reference's own `arccos` offset;
the energy at the target agrees on all three to 1.1e-13. The price is the step count: 60 to 63 outer steps
and 3 to 5 times the mixing loop's time on the canted pair, and a run given fewer steps than it needs
hands over with a warning rather than in silence.

**The direction scheme's reference has a floor of its own.** `FSM_TOL=1e-12` did not hold `atomic fsm
direction`: the loop stopped at 90.000001 degrees with `dm_perp` = 2.5e-8 mu_B, 1.0e-9 Ry off in its own
raw total. `MagneticField.site_residuals` reads the angle as `arccos` of a cosine, and a cosine within one
ulp of 1 resolves no angle below `sqrt(2 x 1.1e-16)` = 1.5e-8 rad, 8.5e-7 degrees, so the loop's test
passes there whatever the tolerance. This is the loop's own resolution, not Newton's; for a tighter
reference the residual would be read as `atan2(|m x t|, m . t)`. Not changed here (library code of the
mixing loop, outside this item).

**What stays refused**, each with its test in `tests/unit/test_residual_fields.py`:

- `reducebf < 1` (`test_a_decaying_field_is_refused`). The scale's own map, `s -> reducebf s`, has a
  well-defined root, `s = 0`, but it is the field-free problem: a residual solver carrying the scale
  would solve the run without the field, while the loop stops at whatever field is left (`fields.py`
  `FADED_FIELD`: 1.8e-3 Ry left and 2.2e-8 Ry of error at `reducebf = 0.9`), so the two differ by that
  field and not by `conv_thr`, and the field's one job, breaking a symmetry on the way, would be lost. The
  message now says so and names the two ways round: the mixing loop, or the residual solver without the
  field from a starting state that already breaks the symmetry. No D22 run was spent on it. The line
  cannot be reached for a feedback scheme: `build_system` refuses `fsm`, `atomic fsm` and `atomic fsm
  direction` with `reducebf < 1` first (`system/builder.py`, a `ValueError`, as the verifier's probe in
  `ver/units.log` shows), so what this refusal covers is a field the loop holds, `B_field` or a penalty.
- `fsm_update = 'secant'` (`test_a_secant_controller_is_refused`): a controller with memory, the previous
  (field, moment) pair, stepping on converged pairs only, so not a function of the state. Its zero is the
  same field, and Newton's Jacobian is the susceptibility it estimates.
- `fsm` in a noncollinear run (`test_an_unmeasured_feedback_regime_is_refused`): not measured. The
  per-atom schemes in a collinear run never reach the residual, `build_system` refuses them.
- a feedback field beside a term neither measured cell carries
  (`test_a_feedback_field_beside_an_unmeasured_term_is_refused`, `residual._refuse_unmeasured_feedback`,
  added in the fix round after the verifier's probes found the first three accepted, the meta-GGA added
  beside them on the same reasoning): an occupation other than a smearing (tetrahedra of any kind,
  `from_input`; `fixed` with `nspin = 2` needs `tot_magnetization` and was not probed), a Hubbard U, a
  meta-GGA, and `K_POINTS gamma` half-sphere storage. The gamma pair is reachable only for `fsm` in a
  collinear norm-conserving run with `nosym`: a spinor or ultrasoft run at `K_POINTS gamma` is substituted
  to the full sphere at input (`gamma_only` reads False on the canted pair), which is an ordinary
  one-point k-set, so the verifier's `atomic fsm` with `K_POINTS gamma` was never on the half sphere and
  stays admitted. The converse, the same cells without the constraint still building a residual, is
  `test_the_unmeasured_terms_pass_without_a_feedback_field`.
- a noncollinear Hubbard U, **for every residual run**, field or not
