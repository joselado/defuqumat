## What is missing, as found

The entry's missing term is right in substance, and reading `response/elastic.py`
(`elastic_constants`, docstring and `_require_a_closed_grid`) says why it is not a
rank-4 average.

`C_ijkl` is one `jvp` of the stress, and the stress is `jax.grad` of
`forces/energy.py:energy_at` with the density **built inside** the functional, because
the stress is the total derivative: `dE/dx = d_x E|_rho + (dE/d rho)(d rho/dx)|_psi`, and
for a strain the second term does not vanish (the density carries `1/Omega`). The
docstring records 671 GPa the other way against 209, on silicon whose `C_11` is 166.

On a wedge the functional symmetrises that density as a **scalar**, so inside the
`jvp` along one strain direction `e_kl` the tangent `d rho` is projected onto its fully
symmetric part, which a strain that breaks the symmetry does not have. A wedge needs the
tangent symmetrised as one component of a **rank-2 tensor field**, mixing the six strain
directions, and that cannot be written per column:

- each `jvp` sees one strain direction, so a symmetriser on its tangent has no access to
  the other five;
- a `custom_jvp` on the scalar symmetriser has the same problem;
- symmetrising the finished `C_ijkl` as a rank-4 tensor (what `electrostriction` does
  for its own half) completes the per-k terms but not the Hartree and exchange-correlation
  terms, which are quadratic in the full-zone `d rho`.

What would work is either a `jvp` batched over the six strain directions with a
symmetriser that acts on that batch axis as a rank-2 index, or computing the six tangents
outside the functional, symmetrising them together (as the strain response's
`symmetrize_directional` already does), and contracting. Both restructure the elastic
assembly, which is beyond this unit's budget.
