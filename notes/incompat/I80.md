# I80. Phonon at `q != 0` x nonlinear core correction

Status: done, both routes (whole and k-chunked), norm-conserving, one spin channel, which is
everything else the route admits. The refusal in `require_a_two_sphere_regime` is removed.

## The number

The check is the entry's identity: a phonon at a commensurate `q` is the `Gamma` phonon of the
supercell `q` folds onto. Silicon's primitive cell (ibrav 2 vectors written out, `ecutwfc = 16`,
`nosym`, `noinv`, 4x2x2 unshifted grid) at `q = b1/2`, against the cell doubled along `a1` on the
2x2x2 grid, unfolded as `D(q)_(s,t) = Phi_(s0,t0) + e^{i q.a1} Phi_(s0,t1)`. The two FFT grids
are (20,20,20) and (40,20,20), so the supercell's grid is the unit cell's doubled. Measured
2026-10-16 on D22, CPU, one core, one run each (deterministic single runs, no median), logs
`/l/ladovj1/review/incompat/I80/{nocore1,core1,broad2,stream3}.log`.

| dataset | max abs(D_q - D_unfolded), Ry/bohr^2 | guard lifted, no core terms | bare term in, core-core swap left at q=0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Si.pz-vbc` (no core, the floor) | 4.40e-6 | - | - |
| `Si.pz-oncv-nlcc` (ONCV, 0.74 e model core) | **2.79e-6** | 1.26e-1 | 2.79e-6 |
| broad Gaussian core (1 e, rc = 1.6 bohr) | **4.81e-6** | 7.8e-2 | 3.18e-3 |

The force constants are about 0.30. The floor is the Ewald swap that `GAMMA_AGREEMENT` in
`tests/regression/test_phonons_at_q.py` names (the supercell's `Gamma` Hessian carries the ground
state's Ewald sum cut at `4/sqrt(alpha)`, where `d2ionq` uses 5). The middle column is what the
route would give if the refusal were simply removed.

**On the ONCV dataset the identity cannot see the core-core term.** Its model core is compact,
`C(q) - C(0)` at `b1/2` is 9.1e-7 Ry/bohr^2, below the floor, and the matrix with and without the
swap reads 2.79e-6 both times. That is why the third dataset is there: the ONCV dataset with its
`rho_core` replaced by a broad Gaussian (15 per cent of its peak at half the bond). There the
swap is 3.17e-3 and the identity holds to 4.81e-6 with it and 3.18e-3 without it, which is the
number the core-core term is checked by.

`q = 0` through two spheres against the `Gamma` route on the same state (`tr2 = 1e-16` both):
8.8e-7 (ONCV) and 3.0e-7 (broad). `C(0)` is real to 1e-17 and symmetric to 2e-17.

The k-chunked route against the whole route at one k-point a chunk, broad core, `q = b1/2`
(`stream3.log`): **1.40e-11** Ry/bohr^2, both routes 11 passes, frequencies 117.21 (pair),
361.12, 385.67, 516.71 (pair) cm^-1. That is above `test_streamed_phonons.py`'s 1e-11 bound and

## What was missing

The entry says the missing term is `dynmatcc` at `q` (`set_drhoc(xq)`, `dynmatcc.f90:105`). That
is wrong. `dynmatcc.f90` says "Diagonal (q-independent) NLCC contribution" in its own header: it
is the same-atom `int v_xc d^2 rho_core`, built with `drc` at `q0`, and the `set_drhoc(xq, drc)`
at `:105` only prepares the table for later use. The `q` dependence of the core correction in
`ph.x` is in two other places, and the code here lacked three things:

1. **The response**. `addcore` inside `dv_of_drho` (`solve_linter.f90`): the core charge travels
   with its atom, so the solve sees `f_xc drho_core(q)`. The `q` route's bare perturbation
   (`applied_at_q`) had only the local and nonlocal pseudopotential terms. Now
   `core_displacements_at_q` builds `drho_core(q)` as one `jvp` through the positions of
   `pseudo/potentials.core_charge_at_q` (the core table at `|G+q|`, the phase
   `e^{-i(G+q).tau}`) and `dv_core = f_xc drho_core` from `induced_potential_at_q` minus its
   Hartree term; `bare_displacements_at_q` and the k-chunked `prepare` add it as a local operator
   (`local_at_q`). Without it the matrix is 1.26e-1 off.
2. **The cross term** of `dynmat_nlcc.f90`, `int conj(f_xc drho_i) drho_core_j`. With `dv_core`
   in the bare vectors `response_force_constants` contracts it with its measured factor 2 and
   conjugation, so it needs no code of its own. `ph.x` keeps the core term in `dV_scf` instead;
   the fixed point is the same, and the electron-phonon `g = bare + dV_scf` sees it once either
   way.
3. **The core-core term** `int conj(drho_core_i) f_xc drho_core_j`, the rest of `dynmat_nlcc`.
   The `Gamma` frozen Hessian (`jax.hessian` of `E_xc[rho + rho_core(tau)]` at fixed `rho`)
   holds it at `q = 0`; `swap_core` subtracts that and adds it at `q`, as `swap_ewald` does for
   the Ewald sum. This is the term that couples two atoms' cores, which is what the entry's
   physics sentence was about.

Memory: four sets of `3 nat` complex dense grids (`drho_core` and `dv_core` at `q` and at 0), in
host memory on the k-chunked route.
