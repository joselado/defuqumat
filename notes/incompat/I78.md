**The first round's reason for the refusal was wrong**, and the verifier measured it wrong: it said the
starting group's wedge is broken by the first turn. It is not. `OrientationStepper.propose`
(`scf/orientation.py`) builds every step from the torque history alone (a fixed first step along the
torque, then BFGS from an isotropic inverse Hessian, a trust radius), the energy is invariant under the
starting group `G_s`, so the torque is invariant under each operation's action `det(R) R` on a rotation
vector, the inverse Hessian built from invariant pairs commutes with it, and every turn therefore commutes
with `G_s`: the turned texture keeps `G_s` for the whole run, on any cell, and its wedge stays complete.
The one path where this is not automatic is `flat_curvature` (off by default), which projects the
gradient on eigenvectors of the inverse Hessian chosen by `eigh`, and in a degenerate eigenspace those need
not respect the group. The new unit test
`test_the_stepper_keeps_the_starting_group_when_the_torque_respects_it` feeds torques along `y` (the
direction `C2y` and `sigma_y.T` leave alone) through the first step, the BFGS updates and the trust radius,
and asserts every step stays along `y`.

**What separates the two groups is what they pin.** `G_s` holds the moment exactly in its fixed subspace
(`m_y = 0` on this start), so a run started on a symmetric saddle stays there; `{E, I}` acts on every axial
vector as the identity and constrains nothing, while `nosym` and `{E, I}` only drift from that subspace as
far as the stepper's noise and its stopping threshold let them: the final `m_y` on the committed grid is
6e-20 on the starting group, 6.6e-8 under `nosym` and -4.9e-6 mu_B on `{E, I}`. On a cell whose minimum lies outside the starting group's subspace the two would
read differently; neither cell here is one, which is why the 1.85e-13 and the 5.7e-14 cannot tell them
apart. The guard on the starting group therefore stays (the brief: a decision that is the user's is taken
the conservative way), with its message, its comment, the `with_turning_moments`, `turning_moments` and
`orientation_free_symmetries` docstrings and both test docstrings rewritten to give this reason instead of
the false one. The cost is real: `with_turning_moments` takes 10 points where the starting group takes 8
(9 against 6 on the shifted grid).

**That the guard fired before.** At the base commit 126d6ec (`w-inc-I78-base`, `base.log`), the same
symmetric cell with `rotate_moments=True` raised "rotate_moments needs nosym = .true.: ..." with `nk 8
nsym 4`, in 2.4 s.

**What was missing, and where the entry was wrong.** The entry said "rebuilding the magnetic group and the
k-set as the moments turn". That is a different k-set at every step, and the wavefunction store, the
mixer's history, the Davidson warm start and the checkpoints all assume one fixed set, so it is a rewrite
of the loop and was not done. What was missing is the **subgroup that never needs rebuilding**: the
stepper turns every moment by one global rotation `Q`, an operation acts on an axial vector as
`det(R) R`, and it stays a symmetry of `Q m` for every `Q` only if that action commutes with all of
SO(3), so only if `det(R) R = 1`, meaning `R = E` or `R = -E` (inversion), each with any fractional
translation. The texture test is the magnetic filter's, already passed, and a rigid rotation preserves it.
Inversion is a symmetry of the coupled Hamiltonian for any moment direction, so `E(k) = E(-k)` holds along
the whole run and the wedge sum is exact, which is what the 5.7e-14 says.

Implemented as `system/symmetry.py:orientation_free_symmetries` (the filter, applied to the output of
`magnetic_symmetries`), a static field `System.turning_moments` read by `System.symmetry_group` and by
`System._respin_kpoints` (the two places that build the group for a System; `build_system` never sets it),
`System.with_turning_moments()` which rebuilds the k-points as `with_moments` does, and
`Calculator.with_turning_moments()`. The FFT grid is unchanged, since `basis/builder.py` sizes it from
`find_symmetries` and not from the run's group. `_refuse_rotating_moments` now accepts `nosym` **or** a
System with `turning_moments`.

**Decisions taken the conservative way, for the user.**

- The operations `{+-E | t}` followed by time reversal also act as a constant (`-1`) on every moment and so
  commute with `Q`; they are symmetries of every turned texture in principle, but no cell here exercised
  them under the stepper, so they are dropped. Keeping them would reduce the k-set of a PT-symmetric
  antiferromagnet further.
- The group is not chosen automatically by `run_scf(rotate_moments=True)`: the k-set is part of the
  `System`, and a Calculator whose result came from a different k-set than its own would hand downstream
  methods (DOS, response) a wedge of the wrong group. So the user asks for it with
  `with_turning_moments()`, and the refusal names the method.
- **The question for the user**, one pick:
  1. Lift the refusal for the starting group: a symmetric NC or US run turns on its own wedge (8 points on
     cobalt, measured 1.85e-13 and 2.0e-13 Ry from `nosym` on two cells), at the price that the moment
     stays exactly in the group's fixed subspace; `with_turning_moments` stays as the option that pins
     nothing.
  2. Keep requiring `with_turning_moments` or `nosym` (now in the code): 10 points, nothing pinned, measured
     5.7e-14 and 2.8e-14 Ry from `nosym`.
  3. Pick automatically: the starting group when its fixed subspace contains every orientation the user
     cares about, which needs a way to say so, and is not built.
  A second, smaller question: should a `&system` keyword set `turning_moments` from an input file, or
  should `get_scf(rotate_moments=True)` on a symmetric Calculator rebuild itself?
- **PAW keeps the refusal under the turning group** (`nosym` PAW runs as before). By reading, the PAW
  branch of the stepper should be complete on the wedge: the turned `becsum` and density go through
  `Calculation.becsum` and `Calculation.density` (`scf/driver.py`, the stepper's `if calculation.is_paw`
  block), which apply `_becsum_symmetry` and the density symmetriser, and the one-centre torque
  (`_onecenter_torque`, `_streamed_onecenter_torque`) differentiates `calculation.becsum(...)` or finishes
  each tangent with `finish_becsum`, all linear averages over `{E, I}`, which commute with a spin turn.
  The measurement did not confirm it, and could not have: PAW nickel
  (`ni-tetragonal-relaxed-mae-paw.in` at 45 degrees, `ecutwfc = 45, ecutrho = 360`, the committed 3x3x2,
  10 points against 18, `rotation_start = 1`, harness `review/incompat/I78/harness_paw.py`, log
  `check2-paw.log`, one sample) left both arms far from convergence (`dr2` 0.26 and 2.2 Ry after five
  iterations), 0.0024 Ry and a torque of 1e-2 apart after five, and 14.5 Ry apart after twelve, with the
  `nosym` arm's torque at 0.41 Ry/rad at the sixth step, so the trajectory is unstable and a fixed-count
  comparison is not a check. Even the first iteration, before any turn, reads 1.45e-5 against 1.97e-5
  Ry/rad, but that iteration is diagonalised at the first threshold (1e-2), where the torque is the
  eigensolver's noise, so it does not separate a missing term from noise either. A PAW check needs a
  frozen-density torque at a tight threshold on both k-sets, or a PAW cell on which Route C converges;
  the guide already says PAW nickel stalls with the torque near 1e-4. Test:
  `tests/unit/test_rotating_moments.py::test_a_paw_run_on_the_turning_group_is_refused_and_nosym_paw_is_not`.
- `Calculator.with_turning_moments()` on a calculator that has already run `get_scf()` hands the old
  result, on the starting group's wedge, as `starting_from` to a run on the new wedge, as `with_kpoints`
  does; that path was not exercised here.
