# I112. `rotate_moments` x DFT+U: partial. The torque with a U is the gradient and the end state is a minimum on its branch; the entry's check, the minimum of the relaxed anisotropy, is not met, because the occupation matrix has several branches on the test cell

**The number.** The torque the step acts on, with DFT+U's share added (`_hubbard_torque`), against the
finite-difference derivative of the band free energy at a frozen input. Tetragonal cobalt with the
coupling (`tests/data/qe/co-tetragonal-relaxed-mae.in`, ultrasoft fully-relativistic PBE, 3x3x2,
`nosym`) with `HUBBARD {ortho-atomic}`, `U Co-3d 1.0` eV. The converged state along `c` is turned rigidly
by `theta` about `y` (density by `rotate_texture`, `ns` by `rotate_ns`) and diagonalised once at
`diago_thr_init = 1e-12`. The band free energy of that one diagonalisation, `F = sum wg e - TS`, has the
derivative `sum f <dH/dtheta>`, which is minus the recorded `y` torque, and a central difference shares
no machinery with the torque. On D22 (worktree `/l/ladovj1/w-inc-rotate-v2` at 02dc69c3, harness
`review/incompat/rotate/harness_u3.py`, log `u3b.log`, one sample each, CPU, one core):

| `theta` (deg) | `h` (deg) | `dF/dtheta` (Ry/rad) | `-G_y`, grid + U (Ry/rad) | relative error | U share off: `-G_y` grid only | relative error, U off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45 | 2.0 | 2.20065e-4 | 2.20117e-4 | -2.37e-4 | 1.04552e-4 | +0.5248 |
| 45 | 0.5 | 2.20114e-4 | 2.20117e-4 | -1.50e-5 | 1.04552e-4 | +0.5250 |
| 20 | 2.0 | 1.08006e-4 | 1.08032e-4 | -2.38e-4 | 5.15648e-5 | +0.5225 |
| 20 | 0.5 | 1.08030e-4 | 1.08032e-4 | -1.55e-5 | 5.15648e-5 | +0.5227 |

**The convergence parameter is the stencil `h`**: the error falls by 15.8 and 15.3 from `h = 2` to
`0.5`, the factor 16 of a central difference's `h^2`, so what is left at 0.5 degree is the stencil and
not the torque. **With the term switched off** (DFT+U's share left out, `HUBBARD_TORQUE = False`) the
torque is half the gradient, +0.52 at both angles and both steps: the Hubbard potential carries 52 per
cent of the turning force on this cell.

**The end state.** `rotate_moments=True` from 45 degrees, `conv_thr = 1e-12` (`harness_u4.py`, log
`u4.log`, same worktree, one sample each):

| `torque_conv_thr` | iterations | angle from `c` (deg) | `|M|` (mu_B) | total energy (Ry) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-8 | 206 | 38.55801 | 1.778346 | -74.36116529951221 |
| 1e-9 | 243 | 38.55740 | 1.778346 | -74.36116529951220 |
| 1e-10 | 269 | 38.55758 | 1.778346 | -74.36116529951123 |

The angle moves by 6e-4 degree and the energy by 1e-12 Ry over two decades of the threshold.

- **Fixed point** (`harness_u3.py`, `u3b.log`): a plain SCF from the 1e-9 end state's density and `ns`
  converged in 10 iterations, moved the moment by 3e-5 degree and the energy by 1.1e-12 Ry.
- **Minimum, not saddle, on its branch** (`harness_u6.py`, log `u6.log`, worktree
  `/l/ladovj1/w-inc-rotate-v3` at 71756a3b): the saved end state turned by +2 and -2 degrees and run
  plain went back, 40.55 to 38.593 degrees in 46 iterations (energy +6.2e-11 Ry, torque -1.9e-7 still
  restoring) and 36.56 to 38.556 in 54 (energy within 7e-13). Both sides restore, so the step stopped
  at a minimum of the orientation on the branch of `ns` it sits on.

**The two term-off arms of the endpoint do not discriminate, and this is said here in those words.**
From 45 degrees, `torque_conv_thr = 1e-9` (`harness_u5.py`, log `u5.log`):

| arm | iterations | angle (deg) | energy (Ry) |
|---|---|---|---|
| both terms on | 243 | 38.55740 | -74.3611652995122 |
| `ns` not turned with the step (state and mixer history) | 235 | 38.55533 | -74.3611652995137 |
| DFT+U's share of the torque left out | 364 | 38.55628 | -74.3611652995140 |

All three reach the same state to 2e-3 degree and 2e-12 Ry. The reason is that the endpoint is fixed
by self-consistency: `E_U` and `E_xc` are each invariant under a global spin turn, so at `in = out`
each share of the torque is zero on its own, and the loop stops where the plain loop would stop
anyway. A confirming endpoint could not have read differently, so it is not the check of either term
(the trap "a confirming number counts only if the alternative hypothesis would have read
differently"). The discriminating measurement is the frozen identity above, and for the torque share
it reads +0.52 off against -1.5e-5 on. With the share left out the step is half as long as it should
be and BFGS takes 121 more iterations to learn the curvature it hides. **The `ns` turn bought nothing
measurable on this cell** (235 iterations without against 243 with), and on this dataset it is not
exact: turning the converged states by `rotate_spinors` and rebuilding `ns` differs from
`rotate_ns(ns)` by 6.6e-4 at a turn of 0.3 rad (change 6.9e-2) and 5.4e-4 at `(0.2, -0.1, 0.25)`
(change 2.6e-2), on a converged state whose own `ns` it reproduces to 0. The cause is the one the
docstring of `rotate_ns` names: on an ultrasoft dataset with the coupling `wfcU = S phi` and `S`
carries `qq_so`, which is not spin-rotation invariant. On a norm-conserving dataset the turn is exact
(`tests/unit/test_rotating_moments.py::test_the_occupation_matrix_turns_with_the_states_as_conj_u_n_u_transpose`).
It stays, being harmless here and exact where the projectors allow it, but it is not sold as a gain.

**Why the entry's check is not met.** The entry asked for "the minimum of the relaxed anisotropy,
which accepts a U". On this cell at `U = 1` eV there is no single-valued `E(theta)` to take a minimum
of. Six self-consistent or descending states were found (the six rows below), spanning 1.58e-3 Ry,
about fifty times the anisotropy of the same cell at `U = 0` (3.1054e-5 Ry, I113); the record first
said five states in 9e-4 Ry, corrected at integration from the verifier's count:

| start | angle (deg) | `|M|` (mu_B) | energy (Ry) | state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| atomic, along `c` | 0 | 1.781400 | -74.36094894587 | converged, 23 iterations |
| atomic, along `x` | 90 | 1.781576 | -74.36101106170 | converged, 25 iterations |
| atomic, at 38.5574 | drifted to 46.27 | 1.781212 | -74.36029230646 | 200 iterations, accuracy 5.7e-7, torque 5.9e-5 |
| `rotate_moments` from 45 | 38.5574 | 1.778346 | -74.36116529951 | converged, a minimum on its branch |
| end state turned rigidly to 0, plain | drifted to 34.47 | 1.778310 | -74.36137662972 | 200 iterations, accuracy 7.4e-8, torque 2.1e-5 |
| end state turned rigidly to 90, plain | drifted to 71.19 | 1.779184 | -74.36187566856 | 200 iterations, accuracy 8.9e-7, torque 1.2e-4, still descending |

The last two were meant as the end state's own branch at 0 and 90 degrees, to fit `K1` and `K2` and
check the stationarity at 38.56 degrees, the way I113 does. They are not on that branch, because the
spin-only turn of `ns` is 1 per cent inexact here and leaves the orbital part where it was, so a turn
of 38.6 or 51.4 degrees lands on a different state; the fit they gave is meaningless and is not
quoted. What they do show is that states 2e-4 and 7e-4 Ry below the end state exist, against
accuracies of 1e-7 to 1e-6. The `|M|` of the end state, 1.7783, against 1.7814 on the atomic starts,
is a cheap sign that a different branch was reached. So what `rotate_moments` with a U is measured to
do is what it does without one, a minimum of the orientation on the branch it starts on, and **what it
is not measured to do, and on this cell does not do, is find the lowest state**. That is the physics of
DFT+U's metastable occupations rather than a missing term of the step, and a plain SCF has it too; the
entry's check needs a cell where the branch is unique, which this one at 1 eV is not. A 3 eV `U` did
not converge a plain SCF along `c` or along `x` in 150 iterations (`u1.log`, accuracy 1e-10 against
1e-12, with `E(x) - E(z) = 2.5e-2` Ry, so the branches are already far apart there), and that log stops
after those lines with no `# exit` line, cause not recorded; the larger `U` was not pursued.

**What stays refused**, each with its test in `tests/unit/test_rotating_moments.py`:

- a Hubbard `U` on a PAW dataset: the turned `becsum` there carries a shift measured on the turned
  states and `ns` would want the same, which is neither written nor checked;
- a fixed tensor moment (`hubbard.constraints`): the constraint holds the shell's spin structure.

**That the guard fired before.** At master 6c5b3a36 `_refuse_rotating_moments` raised
`NotImplementedError("rotate_moments with a Hubbard U: the occupation matrix ns is mixed with the
density ...")` for any `calculation.is_hubbard`; the refusal stub test now asserts a U passes on a
norm-conserving or ultrasoft dataset and that the PAW and tensor-moment cases still raise.

**Fast unit tests on D22**: `tests/unit/test_rotating_moments.py` and `tests/unit/test_magnetic_fields.py`
at 71756a3b, 57 passed (`unit2.log`), among them the new
`test_the_packed_mixer_vector_turns_its_ns_block_and_nothing_else`, which checks the one piece no
converged number covers (a misplaced `ns` block in the mixer history would slow the mixer, not move the

**The decision this leaves to the user.** The code as committed keeps the lift. The alternatives are
to keep it and warn at the hand-over when `|M|` of the end state differs from the start's, or to put
the U refusal back by name with this record as its reason.

**User guide**, `docs/guide/anisotropy.md`, "Limits" note, the paragraph on `rotate_moments`. Replace

> It also gives an error for a spin spiral, for a Hubbard $U$, whose occupation matrix would have to
> turn with the density, and for a magnetic field or a constrained moment, which holds the orientation
> that the step moves.

with (this text also carries I113's change, and supersedes the one proposed in `I113.md`):

> It also gives an error for a spin spiral. It takes a magnetic field that you apply with `B_field`,
> and then the run stops where the anisotropy and the energy of the field balance. This needs `nosym`.
> It gives an error for a constrained moment, because the constraint holds the orientation that the
> step moves, and for `reducebf`, because then the field changes during the run. It takes a Hubbard
> $U$ on a norm-conserving or ultrasoft dataset, and the occupation matrix turns with the moments.
> With a Hubbard $U$ the occupation matrix can have more than one self-consistent solution, and the
> run does not look for the lowest one. So the run stops at the easy orientation of the solution it
> starts on, and a different start can give a different solution with a lower energy. It gives an
> error for a Hubbard $U$ on a PAW dataset and for a fixed tensor moment.

**README.** The bullet "Easy orientation of a magnetic texture" may gain, after "converges to the easy
orientation and does not wander around it.": "This also works with an applied field `B_field` and
with a Hubbard $U$. With a $U$ the run finds the easy orientation of the solution it starts on. This
is not always the lowest one." The feature-table row "Easy orientation of a magnetic texture, relaxed"
needs no change of its cells.

## Integration (2026-10-20, integration three of T3, branch `t3-third` onto `incompat-t3`)

Merged with `--no-ff` (unit `rotate`, with I113), no conflict. The verifier confirmed the frozen
identity on five variants (norm-conserving Ni, atomic projectors, a `J`) and refuted two text points,
corrected here: the table of states has six rows spanning 1.58e-3 Ry, not five in 9e-4; and the
Limits sentence must instruct the reader, not only describe.

**The decision**, taken by Fable while the user was away (2026-10-20), on the three options above:
keep the lift; the guide's Limits note says to run from two or three starting orientations and keep
the lowest energy; and the code warns at the end of a `rotate_moments` run with a U when `|M|`
differs from the starting `|M|` by more than 1e-3 mu_B, with the threshold in the warning and in the
guide, and both say that an unchanged `|M|` is not evidence that the lowest solution was found. The
reason given: the torque is measured right; several `ns` solutions exist whether or not the moments
turn; and a warning turns a silent wrong answer into a named one.

**What was written.** `defuqumat/scf/driver.py`: `ROTATE_HUBBARD_MOMENT_TOL = 1e-3` and
`_warn_if_the_hubbard_moment_moved`, called just before `run_scf` returns when `rotate_moments` and
`calculation.is_hubbard`. The starting `|M|` is that of the density handed in (`starting_density` or
`starting_from`); on an atomic start the moment of the input is the `starting_magnetization` guess,
so the first iteration's output moment stands in, and the warning names which one it used. Not
measured: whether it fires on this record's 45-degree run (the harness logged only the end `|M|`,
1.778346, and no per-iteration moment), and on an atomic start the first-iteration `|M|` is not a
converged reference, so the warning can fire there without a change of branch. Fast test
`tests/unit/test_rotating_moments.py::test_a_rotation_with_a_hubbard_u_warns_when_its_moment_moves`
(fires at 1.781400 to 1.778346, silent at a 9e-4 change and without a reference, and the driver's
call site present). Guide: `docs/guide/anisotropy.md` Limits, with the six states, the instruction
and the 1e-3 threshold. README: the bullet gained I112's sentences. Ledger: I112 marked partial in
place.

## The user's amendment (2026-10-20)

**Fable's decision, amended by the user**: the `|M|` warning of a `rotate_moments` run with a U fires
only when the run starts from a density (`starting_from` or `starting_density`, the first of which
reaches `run_scf`'s check as the second). On an atomic start there is no warning. Reason, from the
record above: the atomic start's moment is the `starting_magnetization` guess and the first
iteration's output is not a converged reference, so a warning there could fire without a change of
branch.

**What changed.** `defuqumat/scf/driver.py`: the fallback to the first iteration's output moment is
removed; `hubbard_start_moment` is set only when `starting_density is not None`, and the call passes
it with the reference "the starting state handed in". The helper, its text and
`ROTATE_HUBBARD_MOMENT_TOL = 1e-3` are unchanged, and the docstring records the amendment. Fast test
`tests/unit/test_rotating_moments.py::test_a_rotation_with_a_hubbard_u_warns_when_its_moment_moves`:
fires from a density (1.781400 to 1.778346), silent at a 9e-4 change, silent without a reference (the
atomic start), and the driver's source takes its reference only under `starting_density is not None`
and no longer names "the first iteration's output". Guide: `docs/guide/anisotropy.md` Limits says the
check runs from a density and that an atomic start does not warn. README: unchanged, since its bullet
does not describe the warning. Slow tests that pass through the call site, not run:
`tests/regression/test_anisotropy.py::test_turning_the_moments_with_a_hubbard_u_stops_on_a_fixed_point_of_the_plain_loop`
and `::test_the_torque_with_a_hubbard_u_is_the_derivative_of_the_frozen_band_energy`.
